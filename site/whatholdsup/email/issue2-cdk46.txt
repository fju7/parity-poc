WHAT HOLDS UP
Issue two - 28 August 2026

THE CATEGORY DIFFERENCE

Three drugs of the same class treat the same disease. A guideline
grades one of them above the other two, and says why. No trial has
ever tested one against another.

The NCCN breast cancer guidelines, version 6.2026, cover first-line
treatment of hormone-receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced
breast cancer. Combined with an aromatase inhibitor, ribociclib is
category 1. The same combination with abemaciclib or palbociclib is
category 2A. The guideline states the reason: the overall survival
benefit seen with ribociclib.

Three trials sit behind that grade - MONARCH 3 for abemaciclib,
MONALEESA-2 for ribociclib, PALOMA-2 for palbociclib. Each tested
one drug against endocrine therapy alone. None tested one against
another.

On the endpoint every one of them was powered to measure, nothing
separates them. Progression-free survival hazard ratios of 0.54,
0.57 and 0.58, each from the analysis named on the page. Across the
eight investigator-assessed readouts these trials have published,
every figure falls between 0.52 and 0.58.

The grade turns instead on overall survival, which in these trials
was a secondary endpoint.

  Abemaciclib's hazard ratio for death is 0.80, on an interval
  running 0.64 to 1.02. Ribociclib's is 0.76, on 0.63 to 0.93.
  The point estimates are four hundredths apart. The intervals
  overlap along almost their whole length - 97% of ribociclib's
  lies inside abemaciclib's.

  One interval stopped at 0.93 and the other at 1.02. That is
  the difference between a significant result and a
  non-significant one, and it is what the two drugs are graded
  on.

Overlapping intervals do not prove two effects are equal, and this
piece does not claim they do. It is the guideline that is claiming
a difference. What that claim rests on is where one interval
stopped.

Palbociclib is a different case, and the difference is real. A
hazard ratio of 0.96 on an interval from 0.78 to 1.18 is not a near
miss. What PALOMA-2 established is that it did not demonstrate a
survival benefit - which is less than saying the drug does not
work, and the assessment is careful about the difference. This
piece does not claim palbociclib's result sits where abemaciclib's
does. It does not.


THE SAME DRUG, GRADED BOTH WAYS

Change the partner drug and the grade changes. With fulvestrant
instead of an aromatase inhibitor, ribociclib and abemaciclib are
both category 1 - and the guideline gives the same reason it gives
for the first-line grade: those combinations showed an overall
survival benefit, in MONALEESA-3 and MONARCH 2.

So abemaciclib is a category 1 drug in one setting and a category
2A drug in another, on one standard, consistently applied.

The grade is not a verdict on the molecule. It is a record of which
of that molecule's trials produced a significant survival result.
The guideline says so plainly, and the assessment takes it at its
word.


WHAT THE FULL ASSESSMENT ADDS

Several research programmes have compared all three drugs anyway.
The assessment examines four: a network meta-analysis of seven
randomised trials; P-VERIFY, a US real-world cohort of 9,146
patients; PALMARES-2, an Italian study of 1,982; and an indirect
comparison that rebuilt patient-level data from three of the
trials. It does not claim to have the whole literature - an outside
reviewer found two more that we had missed.

On palbociclib they contradict each other. P-VERIFY reported
survival and progression in two separate papers from the same
cohort, and neither found any separation. PALMARES-2 finds
abemaciclib and ribociclib each ahead of palbociclib on
progression-free survival, on intervals nowhere near the no-effect
line. The full assessment reports that conflict rather than
resolving it, and records one fact a reader weighing the two is
entitled to have: P-VERIFY's full name is the Palbociclib Verifying
Evidence of Real-world Impact study, and its own poster states that
it was funded by Pfizer, which makes palbociclib. That does not make
its findings wrong, and the assessment draws no inference from it - and
it says, in the same breath, that PALMARES-2's own declaration of
interests is behind a wall we could not open, so neither study is
offered as the disinterested one.

On the one comparison the category difference is actually about -
abemaciclib against ribociclib - they do not disagree at all.
P-VERIFY puts it at 0.97 on 0.82 to 1.14 for survival. PALMARES-2,
the study that did separate palbociclib, puts it at 0.91 on 0.73 to
1.14 for progression. Eleven thousand patients, two endpoints, and
not one of them can tell those two drugs apart. The guideline grades
them apart.

The assessment also sets out what each drug actually costs a
patient, from the labels. Ribociclib requires cardiac monitoring.
Abemaciclib carries a labelled thrombosis warning and causes
diarrhoea in 81 to 90 percent of patients. Palbociclib causes
grade 3 or worse neutropenia in two thirds. Three different
burdens, and none of them is what the category measures.

And it records what we tested and could not stand up: three
separate criticisms of the guideline, all three withdrawn before
publication.

WHAT HOLDS UP
Issue one - 27 August 2026

THE MELANOMA TRIAL THAT SUCCEEDED WITHOUT SHOWING ITS WORK

On 19 August, Merck and Moderna announced that a large Phase 3
trial of a personalised mRNA cancer therapy met both of its
endpoints in melanoma. Several outlets called it a landmark. On
the evidence so far it may well be.

The announcement reports no numerical results. It says the trial
met both prespecified endpoints - a real finding, from 1,137
patients in a double-blind trial - and gives no hazard ratio, no
interval, no percentage. The figures in the headlines, a 49%
reduction in the hazard of recurrence or death and 59% in the
hazard of distant metastasis or death, come from a different
study: an open-label trial of 157 patients that first reported
in 2023.

Survival is the question that stays open. In that earlier trial,
five-year overall survival rests on fourteen deaths - seven in
each arm.

  Fourteen deaths. The hazard ratio is 0.471, and the interval
  around it runs from 0.165 to 1.345 - from a rate of death
  about a sixth of the comparison group's, to about 35% higher.
  Too wide to separate a large benefit from a small harm.

None of this means the treatment does not work. It probably
does. It means the public has not been shown how well.


WHAT THE FULL ASSESSMENT ADDS

The specialist trade press largely got this right. Practical
Dermatology, Dermatology Times, Pharmacy Times, OncoDaily and
Targeted Oncology all said plainly that no Phase 3 efficacy
numbers had been released, and traced the 49% and 59% to the
earlier trial.

What none of them did was explain what follows from that. Why
figures from a smaller, unblinded trial cannot be read as the
size of the new one's effect. What fourteen deaths can and
cannot establish. What the absolute numbers look like beside the
relative ones - 68.8% recurrence-free at five years against
49.1%, a gap of about twenty percentage points.

And one thing that reached almost nobody: the earlier trial
enrolled stage IIIB-IV, while the Phase 3 reaches down into
stage IIB. Whether that lowers the absolute benefit is genuinely
disputed, and the full assessment sets out both sides.

THE MELANOMA TRIAL THAT SUCCEEDED WITHOUT SHOWING ITS WORK

On 19 August, Merck and Moderna announced that a large Phase 3
trial of a personalised mRNA cancer therapy met both of its
endpoints in melanoma. Several outlets called it a landmark. On
the evidence so far it may well be.

The announcement reports that both endpoints were met, and no
effect size. That both were met is a real finding, from 1,137
patients in a double-blind trial. What is missing is how large
the effect was: no hazard ratio, no interval, no percentage. The
figures in the headlines, a 49% reduction in the hazard of
recurrence or death and 59% in the hazard of distant metastasis
or death, come from a different study: an open-label trial of
157 patients, whose five-year results were presented in June.
Those are mature numbers. They are just not this trial's, and
157 unblinded patients is not 1,137 blinded ones.

Survival is the question that stays open. In that earlier trial,
five-year overall survival - an exploratory endpoint the trial
was never powered to test - rests on fourteen deaths in total.

  Fourteen deaths. The hazard ratio is 0.471 and the interval
  runs from 0.165 to 1.345 - compatible with a hazard of death
  roughly 83% lower than the comparison group's, and with one
  about 35% higher. The magnitude is what the data cannot yet
  establish; the direction points to benefit.

None of this means the treatment does not work. It probably
does. It means the public has not been shown how well.


WHAT THE FULL ASSESSMENT ADDS

Every figure in this issue traces to a company release or a
peer-reviewed paper. Before publishing we read the specialist
coverage of the announcement, and the careful outlets got the
attribution right: the 49% and 59% belong to the earlier trial,
and several said plainly that the Phase 3 numbers had not been
released.

What the full assessment adds is the layer underneath. Why
figures from a smaller, open-label trial - where an investigator
who knows the assignment can influence when a recurrence is
looked for and called - cannot be read as the size of a blinded
trial's effect. What fourteen deaths can establish about
direction, and what they cannot establish about magnitude. And
what the absolute numbers look like beside the relative ones.

It also sets out a question with two defensible answers: the
earlier trial enrolled stage IIIB-IV, while the Phase 3 reaches
down into stage IIB. Whether that lowers the benefit those
patients can expect is genuinely disputed, and the assessment
gives both sides.

WHAT HOLDS UP
Issue one · 26 August 2026

THE MELANOMA TRIAL THAT SUCCEEDED WITHOUT SHOWING ITS WORK

On 19 August, Merck and Moderna announced that a large trial of a
personalised mRNA cancer therapy worked in melanoma. Several outlets
called it a landmark. It probably is.

The announcement contains no results from that trial. Not one number
describing how well the treatment worked. The figures in the headlines
-- a 49% reduction in recurrence, 59% in distant spread -- belong to a
different, much smaller study: an open-label trial of 157 patients that
first reported in 2023. The trial actually being announced enrolled
1,137 and has published nothing.

That matters most for the endpoint people actually care about --
whether anyone lives longer. At five years, that rests on an analysis
the companies label only "n=14".

  Fourteen events. The range around that figure runs from an 84%
  reduction in the risk of death to a 35% increase. It is consistent
  with the therapy preventing five deaths in six, and with it doing
  harm.

None of this means the treatment doesn't work. It probably does. It
means the public has not been shown how well, and the numbers being
repeated are carrying more weight than they can bear.


WHERE OUR READING DIFFERS

We read every article on the announcement we could reach -- seventeen
of them. Five said plainly that no Phase 3 numbers had been released:
FierceBiotech, BioPharma Dive, Dermatology Times, TechTimes and Life
Science Daily.

The oncology specialists did not. OncLive, AJMC, Targeted Oncology,
ecancer, The ASCO Post, BioPharm International and CancerNetwork each
filled the gap with the older trial's hazard ratios without telling
readers the new trial had produced none.

We expected that the other way round. On this particular point the
general business press was more careful than the specialist press.

And not one of the seventeen reported the event count beneath the
survival figure -- nor did the June coverage of the five-year data,
though the companies disclosed it themselves.

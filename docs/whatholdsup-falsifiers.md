# Recorded falsifiers — every claim that states what would change its mind

**Generated 10 September 2026 by `falsifiers.py`. Read from the records; nothing here is typed.**

This publication's method is that a claim states what would change its mind. It
has recorded **54** of them and checked **none**.

**That is the FORM of falsifiability without the act** — a control described and
never performed, at scale, in the place the epistemics rests. Each row below is a
decision somebody made, with the condition that would overturn it written down at
the time, and no record anywhere of anyone returning to ask whether that condition
has come true.

The `last checked` column reads **never** for all 54. That is the finding, not a
gap in this table.

## They are not one kind, and the split decides what is buildable

| | count |
|---|---|
| **falsifiable from this repository alone** — a string in held bytes, a mark still passing, the page still saying what it said | **18** |
| **requires looking at the world** — a trial releasing an effect size, a paper appearing, an erratum being published | **2** |
| both, or unclear from the wording | **34** |

**That matters for what happens next.** The reason given for not building a
checker — *"a falsifier like 'if the Phase 3 releases an effect size' needs
someone to look at the world"* — is true of 2 of them and **not true of 18**.
Those 18 name a string, a file, a mark or a sentence in this repository, and a
check could evaluate them tonight.

The world-facing ones are the harder problem and stay next issue's work. Splitting
them is the first step, because a single number hid a buildable majority behind an
unbuildable minority — which is failure 18, decompose before deferring, on a pile
that was deferred whole an hour ago.

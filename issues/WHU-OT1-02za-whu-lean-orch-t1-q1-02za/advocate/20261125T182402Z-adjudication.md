## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — SERIOUS / CHANGES

**Type.** MISSTATES

**We say.** The source, a systematic review titled "Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low‐ and middle‐income countries: A systematic review," is supplied here only as a title with no findings or certainty language, so no conclusion about effectiveness can be attributed to it [1].

**They object.** The review is a full 28-study Campbell review with quantified findings and explicit certainty language, so the claim that nothing about effectiveness can be attributed to it is wrong, and it contradicts the page's own other sentences that cite it.

**Their document says.** Among the 18 studies included in the analysis of intervention effects on ‘Skills for learning’, 12 interventions had a significant effect.
  — *Abstract, results summary (paragraph beginning 'The second most common category of intervention was ‘Accessible learning environments’')*

**Why it matters.** The sentence says no effectiveness conclusion can be drawn. The review states one, with graded certainty: emerging support, effects on several skill outcomes, and mostly low confidence. A reader would wrongly think the source is silent.

MERIT:  yes — The source text reports that 12 of 18 'Skills for learning' interventions had a significant effect, with significant effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy, and that 'All but one study received an overall rating of low confidence,' so the sentence's claim of no findings is wrong, and a supported statement of those findings is better than cutting it.
EFFECT: corrects
DID:    the sentence is to be replaced word for word with: "A 2025 Campbell systematic review found that a range of individual-level interventions improve educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries, with significant effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy, though all but one included study received an overall rating of low confidence in study findings [1]."; ruled by ruling model claude-opus-5-5, prompt 388a27cee450


### S001-02 — SERIOUS / NARROWS

**Type.** MISSTATES

**We say.** Only one study achieved the aim of improving attendance, enrolment and completion by equipping mainstream settings for children with disabilities, and it did so by improving teacher knowledge and attitudes about inclusive education [1].

**They object.** The review says the one study improved teacher knowledge and attitudes. It also says no study looked at attendance, enrolment or completion outcomes, so the page overstates what was achieved.

**Their document says.** No study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion of people with disabilities in education.
  — *Summary/discussion section: first sentence of the paragraph beginning 'No study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion'*

**Why it matters.** The measured result was a change in teacher knowledge and attitudes, an intermediate condition for inclusion. No attendance, enrolment or completion outcome was measured. The page presents an unmeasured end outcome as achieved.

MERIT:  yes — The source says the one study improved 'teacher knowledge and attitudes about inclusive education' and also states 'No study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion,' so the sentence cannot claim those outcomes were improved; but the teacher-attitude finding is supported and need not be cut.
EFFECT: corrects
DID:    the sentence is to be replaced word for word with: "One study that sought to improve attendance, enrolment and completion by equipping mainstream settings for children with disabilities improved teacher knowledge and attitudes about inclusive education, but no study measured attendance, enrolment or completion outcomes themselves [1]."; ruled by ruling model claude-opus-5-5, prompt 388a27cee450


### S001-03 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Studies concerned with 'Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education' showed a moderately significant effect [1].

**They object.** The page omits the review's own caveats on this pooled effect: only four studies, very high heterogeneity, and possible publication bias.

**Their document says.** Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education d = 0.35 (0.12 to 0.58) k = 4 n = 401 I² = 86% Egger's test 2.01 ( t = 1.43, p = 0.29) Moderate effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially moderate publication bias
  — *Section 5.3 Effects of interventions, Table 14 Effects of interventions*

**Why it matters.** This is the only systemic-level pooled estimate in the review. The page states it without k = 4 or I² = 86%, so it reads as firmer than the authors present it. The page's generic 'heterogeneity is high' sentence is not tied to this result.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "Studies concerned with 'Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education' showed a moderately significant effect [1].": "For 'Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education', the review reports a pooled effect of d = 0.35 (0.12 to 0.58) across four studies (n = 401, I² = 86%), which the authors describe as a 'Moderate effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially moderate publication bias' [1]."

## S006 — A Guide for ensuring inclusion and equity in education
*Advocate: the authors of A Guide for ensuring inclusion and equity in education*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S006-04 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The guide also warns that inclusion efforts that depend on practices imported from special education tend to foster new and more subtle forms of segregation within mainstream settings [6].

**They object.** The sentence is accurate, but without the guide's own statement that special schools and units have a clear role in promoting inclusion, it reads as a rejection of special provision.

**Their document says.** There is a clear role for special provision, such as special schools and units, in promoting inclusion and equity in education
  — *Dimension 3, Key feature 3.4 (Structures and systems)*

**Why it matters.** The guide's warning is aimed at importing special-education practices into mainstream classrooms. It does not oppose special provision as such, and it asks for links between special and mainstream provision. A reader of the page alone could conclude the guide opposes special-education resources. That would matter if the page assesses what the guide says about children with disabilities.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "The guide also warns that inclusion efforts that depend on practices imported from special education tend to foster new and more subtle forms of segregation within mainstream settings [6].": "The guide also lists among the key features of its framework for analysing inclusion and equity policy that 'there is a clear role for special provision, such as special schools and units, in promoting inclusion and equity in education', though it states this as a policy feature rather than as evidence from an evaluated programme [6]."


### S006-05 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The guide cites strong evidence of the potential of approaches that encourage cooperation between students, and says this evidence suggests such practices can be effective in supporting learners with disabilities, although orchestrating them requires skill [6].

**They object.** The page presents the guide's cooperative-learning evidence as being about learners with disabilities. The guide frames it as evidence for all learners, with disability as one example among several vulnerable groups.

**Their document says.** In particular, there is strong evidence of the potential of approaches that encourage cooperation between students for creating classroom conditions that can both maximize participation, while at the same time achieving high standards of learning for all members of a class (Johnson and Johnson, 1989). Furthermore, this evidence suggests that such practices can be effective in supporting the involvement of all learners who are facing vulnerable situations; examples include those who are new to a class, learners from different cultural and language backgrounds, and those with disabilities.
  — *Passage beginning 'Research indicates that encouraging student participation is the best use of available resources' (section on student participation / cooperative learning)*

**Why it matters.** The page's sentence is not wrong, because disability is named. But it narrows a general claim about participation and learning for all learners into a disability-specific one. That affects how much weight the page can put on this evidence for disability in low-resource settings. The page's separate point that the guide does not tie the evidence to low- and middle-income countries is correct.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers

### S007-06 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Before the review, its protocol noted that evidence on which interventions are effective in meeting international goals for disability and education had not been established [7].

**They object.** The sentence reads as if little or no evidence existed. The protocol says an evidence and gap map had already found 46 education-outcome studies, and that the real gap was study quality, especially impact evaluation.

**Their document says.** The EGM included 46 studies related to education outcomes.

**Why it matters.** 'Not established' is accurate. A reader could still take it to mean there was no evidence base. The protocol describes an existing but thin and low-quality base, and presents the review as the way to appraise and synthesise it.

OMISSION: add

### S007-07 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The protocol also noted that past evidence syntheses had found very little literature examining educational outcomes that compared disabled and nondisabled peers in these countries [7].

**They object.** The sentence omits that the protocol treats the disability gap in education as already well documented, and as largest in LMICs. The scarcity concerns comparative literature on interventions, not the existence of the gap.

**Their document says.** Evidence suggests that the gap in educational attainment for people with and without disabilities is greatest in LMIC.

**Why it matters.** Read alone, the sentence suggests comparative outcome evidence is missing across the board. The protocol says the gap is consistently shown by population-level reports. What is thin is intervention-evaluation literature with a non-disabled comparison, so the claim holds only in that narrower form.

OMISSION: out_of_scope
DID:      nothing added; out of scope (Prevalence of disability among children in LMICs and the size of the out-of-school gap); listed for readers

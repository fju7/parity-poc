## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** On which kinds work best, these passages give no firm ranking, and the authors say more research is needed to understand which types are most efficacious [1].

**They object.** The review reports a specific comparison on this question, mainstream versus specialised delivery, and its result and its caveat are both missing from the page.

**Their document says.** Our review did not reveal any systematic differences between the effectiveness of interventions delivered in mainstream or segregated/specialised settings, but this was likely due to the majority of interventions reporting positive findings.
  — *Section 6.5 Agreements and disagreements with other studies or reviews*

**Why it matters.** 'No firm ranking' is right, but the page gives the impression the review tested nothing on 'which kinds'. It did test delivery setting and found no difference. The authors attribute that to almost everything looking positive, not to the two settings being equally good, so the null cannot be read as parity.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "On which kinds work best, these passages give no firm ranking, and the authors say more research is needed to understand which types are most efficacious [1].": "On inclusive versus segregated provision specifically, the review authors report that their review 'did not reveal any systematic differences between the effectiveness of interventions delivered in mainstream or segregated/specialised settings, but this was likely due to the majority of interventions reporting positive findings' [1]."

### S001-03 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** All but one of the included studies received an overall rating of low confidence in study findings, so the evidence on these programmes is weak [1].

**They object.** The page leaves out the authors' own overall confidence grade for the body of evidence, which is 'low to medium'. That is a softer verdict than the page's bare 'weak'.

**Their document says.** Section 5.2 / 6.3: "Our confidence in the overall findings is low to medium on the basis of our appraisal of the studies." The same section also says the findings "must be interpreted cautiously", given low confidence in study methods, heterogeneity and publication bias.
  — *Section 5.2 (Confidence in study findings); Section 6.3 (Quality of the evidence)*

**Why it matters.** The review itself calls for caution, so 'weak' is defensible. The page omits the authors' own rating for the evidence base, low to medium, and 'weak' reads as harsher than that.

### S001-04 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** MISSTATES

**We say.** Systemic approaches such as inclusive education policy and accessible buildings have not been evaluated in this evidence base, so nothing can be said here about whether they work [1].

**They object.** Policy and built-environment interventions had no studies, but the review did evaluate school-level and environment-level interventions and reports effects for them, so 'systemic approaches ... nothing can be said' overstates the gap.

**Their document says.** No studies represented the intervention categories of ‘Accessibility of built environment and learning materials’ or ‘Inclusive education policies’.
  — *Intervention characteristics, 'Accessible learning environments' sub-categories paragraph (preceding Table 9)*

**Why it matters.** The page's own earlier sentences cite the d = 0.35 estimate and the Good School Toolkit. The closing line is therefore inconsistent with them. The accurate version is that two categories were empty, while a thin and low-confidence environment-level literature (four studies in the pooled estimate) exists and points positive.

MERIT:  yes — The source says only that 'No studies represented the intervention categories of 'Accessibility of built environment and learning materials' or 'Inclusive education policies'', while other environment-level sub-categories (structural interventions, anti-bullying, educational services development) did have studies, so the broader label 'systemic approaches such as' overstates the gap and the reader supplied no replacement text.

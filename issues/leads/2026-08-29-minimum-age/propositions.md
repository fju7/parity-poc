# The falsifiable propositions

Each is a sentence capable of being false, with the thing that would falsify it
stated beside it. None is a claim about whether social media harms teenagers,
and none is a claim about whether the law is right.

---

## PRIMARY

**P1. Every source in Australia's published evidentiary case that makes a
recommendation recommends something other than a legislated minimum age — design
regulation, targeted intervention, or parental limits — and the Impact Analysis
assesses no design-based alternative to the age limit.**

*Falsified by:* any cited source recommending a legislated platform-level
minimum age; or any passage in the Impact Analysis or Explanatory Memorandum
assessing a regulatory option aimed at recommender systems, engagement
optimisation or design.

*Status:* supported for four sources — Orben et al. ("targeted interventions"),
the Surgeon General (age minimums inside a package including design standards,
plus "we do not yet have enough evidence to determine if social media is
sufficiently safe"), Common Sense Media ("putting kids at the center of how
platforms are designed"), Queensland CHO (parental limits, under-14). **The
Explanatory Memorandum is unread and could falsify the second clause.**

---

## SUPPORTING — each independently falsifiable

**P2. Three of the citations assembled as separate support trace to a single
study.**

*Falsified by:* showing endnote 17 of the Surgeon General's Advisory points
somewhere other than Orben, Przybylski, Blakemore & Kievit (2022); or that the
Queensland statement does not rest on the Advisory.

*Status:* endnote 17 confirmed. The Queensland link is from that statement's own
list of primary sources and should be confirmed against its reference list.

**P3. The Impact Analysis states as an established, one-directional finding what
its underlying paper describes as conditional, bidirectional and small.**

*Falsified by:* showing the paper does not condition its causal reading, or does
not report the reverse path, or that the Impact Analysis carries those caveats
somewhere.

*Status:* the paper states that only if five assumptions hold "can this
observational study be said to capture the causal effects"; that lower life
satisfaction predicts later increased use "across all ages and sexes"; and that
"the effect sizes of both paths were small in magnitude". The Impact Analysis
says the study "found that the most detrimental effects ... occur".

**P4. Two of the cited figures measure exposure across the internet generally and
are used to support a remedy that applies only to social media.**

*Falsified by:* showing either figure is social-media-specific in the source.

*Status:* the scope words — "the internet", "online" — are the Impact Analysis's
own. The specific eSafety report cited has not been identified.

**P5. Two cited sources state evidentiary limitations that the Impact Analysis
does not reproduce.**

*Falsified by:* finding those qualifiers reported in the document.

*Status:* the Surgeon General's "not enough evidence to determine if social media
is sufficiently safe"; the Queensland CHO's "these findings have not yet reached
a consensus fully supported by peer-reviewed research". Whether the Impact
Analysis reproduces either is NOT YET CHECKED and must be before publication —
the document does concede an evidence gap in its own voice, and a fair reading
has to weigh that.

**P6. The Parliamentary Library told legislators before the vote that the
evidence for a blanket ban was limited, and the relevant committee declined to
recommend a minimum age.**

*Falsified by:* the Bills Digest saying otherwise, or the committee having
recommended one.

*Status:* both quoted, from a mirrored PDF. Must be re-obtained from aph.gov.au.

## SECOND, SEPARABLE PROPOSITION

**P7. The first study of the law's effects is being reported as more conclusive
than the study itself claims.**

*Falsified by:* the press release matching the paper's language, or the baseline
figure being present.

*Status:* Dr Victoria Baines states on the record that the release "slightly
overstates the findings" and omits the pre-ban baseline; Prof Matt Williams says
it "should not be read as proof that age-based restrictions cannot work". The
BMJ paper itself is unread.

---

## OUT OF SCOPE, EXPLICITLY

- **Whether social media harms teenagers.** Unbounded literature, and the
  evidence-strength comparison was published by Tibor Rutar in April 2026.
- **Whether the law is right.** Turns on values and rights no evidence
  assessment settles.
- **Why anyone did anything.** Motive is quotable when written down and is not
  inferable from conduct. Nothing in these documents speaks to it.
- **Whether the ban worked.** Three months, 408 adolescents, one state,
  self-reported. P7 is about the reporting, not the effect.

## THE CONDITION THAT KILLS THE PIECE

The Explanatory Memorandum, unread, contains an assessment of a design-based
regulatory alternative. That would falsify P1's second clause and remove the
spine. It must be read before drafting, not after.

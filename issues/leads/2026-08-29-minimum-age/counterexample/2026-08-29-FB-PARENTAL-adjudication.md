### CE-01 — role says BROKEN (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** Facebook provided parents with no means to supervise or restrict what their teenage children did on the platform for the first fifteen years of the platform's existence.

**What breaks it.** Facebook launched Messenger Kids in December 2017 — just 13 years after Facebook's 2004 founding — with explicit, built-in parental controls: parents fully controlled the contact list, set up accounts, and approved every contact a child could communicate with, all managed through a parental controls panel inside the main Facebook app. This is a parent-facing supervision and restriction tool on a Facebook-branded platform, within the claimed 15-year window.

**Citation.** https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/introducing-messenger-kids-a-new-app-for-families-to-connect/ ; https://www.cnbc.com/2017/12/04/facebook-launches-messenger-kids.html ; https://venturebeat.com/mobile/facebook-messenger-kids-parental-controls-guides/

**Their words.** “"Facebook is launching a kids messaging app that gives parents control over who can chat with their children." (CNBC, Dec 4 2017); "Parents fully control the contact list and kids can't connect with contacts that their parent does not approve." (Meta official blog, Dec 2017)”

**We inherited this.** The original says: Unknown without seeing the source article, but this type of claim is commonly inherited from litigation complaints or advocacy reports that say Facebook provided 'no meaningful' or 'no effective' parental controls on the main Facebook teen experience — a narrower and more defensible claim than 'no means … for the first fifteen years.' The narrower, accurate version would be: Facebook provided no parental supervision tools on the main Facebook platform for teens (as distinct from Messenger Kids) until November 2023, when it brought parental supervision tools to Facebook proper.

**Does it change the conclusion.** This breaks the sentence, not merely narrows it. The claim is a universal negative ('no means … for the first fifteen years'). Messenger Kids launched in December 2017, which is within the 15-year window (Facebook launched in February 2004; 15 years would run to February 2019). The tool was explicitly a parental-control mechanism on a Facebook-branded product. Even if one argues Messenger Kids targeted under-13s rather than 'teenagers,' the sentence says 'no means' at all — and the 2021 October announcement (reported by Atlanta Journal-Constitution) that Facebook was 'planning to introduce controls for adults of teens on an optional basis so that parents or guardians can supervise what their teens are doing online' also falls within the 15-year window, further breaking the claim. Additionally, Meta's own timeline page confirms parental supervision tools came to Instagram (a Meta/Facebook company) in March 2022, still within 15 years of Facebook's 2004 launch. The piece's broader conclusion — that Facebook was slow and reluctant to give parents tools — may well survive, but this specific sentence, as written, does not.

### CE-02 — role says BROKEN (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** Facebook has never resisted, opposed or lobbied against proposals to give parents control over their teenagers' use of the platform.

**What breaks it.** Multiple well-sourced reports confirm that Meta (Facebook's parent) actively lobbied against the Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA) — a bill that would, among other things, require platforms to equip parents with tools to control their teenagers' privacy settings and online experience. Senator Marsha Blackburn publicly accused Meta of spending $20 million lobbying against KOSA in the first nine months of one year alone, deploying 87 lobbyists. Reuters reported that Meta had been 'dropping its efforts to oppose KOSA' only after negotiating for legal immunity language to be inserted into the bill — confirming prior opposition was real and documented.

**Citation.** https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/meta-lobbies-congress-immunity-from-lawsuits-alleging-online-harm-children ; https://conservativeladiesofamerica.substack.com/p/meta-spent-millions-fighting-kosa ; https://cryptobriefing.com/meta-drops-opposition-kids-online-safety-act/ ; https://www.axios.com/2026/08/26/meta-settlement-congress-kids-online-safety ; https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/about/issues/kids-online-safety-act

**Their words.** “"Meta has proposed the language in exchange for dropping its efforts to oppose KOSA, a source told Reuters." (Fox Business / Reuters, June 2026); "the social media giant reverses course on a bill it spent years lobbying against" (Cryptobriefing, June 2026)”

**We inherited this.** The original says: This claim may have been inherited from Meta's own public-facing messaging, which emphasises its voluntary parental control products (Family Center, Teen Accounts, etc.) and frames itself as a proactive partner to parents. That self-description is narrower than the claim: Meta's PR says it supports parents, not that it has never lobbied against legislation mandating parental controls. The gap between those two positions is exactly where the counterexample lives.

**Does it change the conclusion.** This breaks the sentence entirely. The claim is a universal negative ('has never resisted, opposed or lobbied against'). The documented record shows Meta actively opposed KOSA for years — a bill whose core provisions include mandatory parental control tools over teenagers' platform use. Senator Blumenthal's office noted that bill sponsors faced 'aggressive lobbying' from Big Tech companies. The Axios report confirms Meta 'consistently tops other tech companies in lobbying lawmakers' and had previously opposed KOSA before dropping that opposition in 2026 as part of a deal. This is not a close call. The piece's broader conclusion — that Facebook has been hostile or indifferent to parental oversight — is actually strengthened, not undermined, by this finding. But the sentence as written is still broken because it says 'never,' when the documented lobbying opposition to KOSA is extensive and on the record.

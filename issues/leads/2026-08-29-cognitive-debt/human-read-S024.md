**What to open:**
https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langas/article/PIIS2468-1253(25)00164-5/abstract

Title: *Endoscopist deskilling: an unintended consequence of AI-assisted
colonoscopy?* — the Comment the journal published alongside Budzyń et al.
There is also later correspondence at PIIS2468-1253(25)00290-0.

**Why you and not me:** thelancet.com returns 403 to machine fetch, PubMed
and ResearchGate rate-limited me, and Europe PMC served its homepage.

**Why it matters:** the colonoscopy study is the strongest thing in the piece
and I have only heard the authors' side. A journal that runs a Comment
alongside a paper is signalling there's an argument. If the Comment raises an
alternative explanation the authors' own eleven limitations don't already
cover, the piece has to carry it — and if it broadly endorses the finding,
that's worth knowing too.

This is lower stakes than the last two requests. If it's paywalled and
annoying, say so and I'll write the piece flagging the Comment as unread,
which is honest but weaker.

## The questions

**1. Who wrote it, and when?** Are they independent of the study authors?

**2. Does it accept the deskilling reading, or resist it?**

**3. What alternative explanations does it raise?** In particular anything the
authors don't already list — they cover the retrospective design, the Polish
screening-programme suspension, missing withdrawal times, no blinding, 19
operators, and the excluded first-100 familiarisation cases.

**4. Does it say anything about mechanism?** Attention, vigilance, search
strategy, complacency — anything about *why* detection would fall.

**5. What does it call for?** The study authors ask for randomised crossover
trials. Does the Comment agree, or want something else?

**6. Its concluding sentences,** as close to verbatim as is easy.

**7. If the correspondence is easy to open too** — who wrote it, what they
object to, and whether the study authors replied.

Rough notes are fine.

# Read request — S017, Shaw & Nave

**What to open:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6097646
Click **Download This Paper** (SSRN allows it free; it may ask you to sign in).

**Full citation:** Steven D. Shaw (Wharton; King's College London) & Gideon Nave
(Wharton), *Thinking—Fast, Slow, and Artificial: How AI is Reshaping Human
Reasoning and the Rise of Cognitive Surrender.* Wharton School Research Paper,
posted 2 February 2026. Working paper, not peer reviewed.

**Why you and not me:** SSRN's abstract page is readable but the PDF isn't
reachable by machine.

**Why it matters:** on preregistration alone this is the most rigorous study in
the whole argument — three preregistered experiments, N = 1,372, 9,593 trials,
with the AI's accuracy randomised. It may be the spine of the piece. I have
read only the abstract, and I won't build on an abstract again.

---

## The questions

**1. The three experiments.** N of each, and what differed between them.

**2. The task.** Cognitive Reflection Test items, adapted how? What did a
participant actually see and do?

**3. How AI accuracy was randomised.** Were participants told it might be
wrong? How often was it wrong?

**4. The critical one — when was performance measured?**
Was accuracy measured *while the AI's answer was on screen*, or on separate
trials with no AI? If people could see the AI's answer when they responded,
this measures deference. If they answered afterwards without it, it measures
something closer to what Liu et al. measure. I need to know which.

**5. Effect sizes.** The abstract gives +25 and −15 percentage points. Are
there standardised effect sizes, and confidence intervals?

**6. Preregistration.** Where are the preregistrations — OSF, AsPredicted? Do
the authors say the reported analyses were the preregistered ones, and do they
flag any exploratory analyses as such?

**7. The moderators.** The abstract says higher trust in AI and lower need for
cognition predicted more surrender. Were those measured before the task or
after?

**8. Limitations.** Quote what they list. In particular, do they say anything
about whether this persists beyond the session?

**9. "Tri-System Theory."** Is this framing load-bearing for the empirical
results, or a wrapper around them? I'd rather report the experiments than the
theory, but I need to know if that's fair to them.

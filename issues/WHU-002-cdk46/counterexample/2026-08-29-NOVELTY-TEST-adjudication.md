### CE-01 — role says BROKEN (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** Nobody has published a comparison of the evidentiary record behind tobacco regulation with the evidentiary record on social media harm to adolescents — effect sizes, consistency, experimental evidence and alternative explanations.

**What breaks it.** A Substack essay published April 2, 2026, titled 'You can't analogize the evidence on social media harms to that on smoking!' (statsandsociety.substack.com), explicitly compares the two evidentiary records across all four dimensions named in the claim — effect sizes, consistency of results, experimental evidence, and alternative explanations — and concludes the two bodies of evidence are not analogous.

**Citation.** https://statsandsociety.substack.com/p/you-cant-analogize-the-evidence-on (published April 2, 2026)

**Their words.** “"there's still no analogizing between the two cases: either in terms of effect sizes or consistency of the results"”

**We inherited this.** The original says: The claim appears to be the author's own assertion, not inherited from a guideline or prior source. However, it may echo a rhetorical move common in the social media harm debate — Haidt and others invoke the tobacco analogy (e.g., Haidt 2024, The Anxious Generation, comparing Big Tech to Big Tobacco) without a formal evidentiary comparison, and critics may have assumed no such comparison existed. The actual narrower truth is that no peer-reviewed journal article has done this comparison in a systematic review format — but the comparison has been published in essay and commentary form.

**Does it change the conclusion.** This breaks the sentence, not the piece's broader conclusion. The counterexample actually agrees with the substantive conclusion that the social media evidence base is weaker than the tobacco evidence base — but it proves that the comparison HAS been published, which directly falsifies the universal negative ('nobody has published'). A piece arguing that social media regulation is premature because the evidence is weaker than tobacco's cannot claim the comparison has never been made; that comparison is precisely the argument being made in the counterexample. The sentence must be retracted or rewritten (e.g., 'Few peer-reviewed papers have published...' or 'No systematic review has formally compared...').

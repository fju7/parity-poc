### CE-01 — role says BROKEN (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** No trial has ever tested abemaciclib against either of the other two, and every comparison this page examined of the two the guideline grades apart finds nothing between them.

**What breaks it.** The claim contains two sub-assertions, and both are broken. (1) On abemaciclib vs. the other two: no head-to-head RCT of abemaciclib against palbociclib or ribociclib has been completed, so that half of the sentence survives — but only barely, because the PALMARES-2 study (NCT06805812), a pre-registered, prospective, multicenter Italian study that enrolled all three CDK4/6 inhibitors in a comparative design, reported in April 2025 that abemaciclib and ribociclib were associated with significantly longer real-world PFS than palbociclib (abemaciclib vs. palbociclib aHR 0.76, p=0.004; ribociclib vs. palbociclib aHR 0.83, p=0.007), directly contradicting the 'nothing between them' half of the claim. (2) On the two 'the guideline grades apart' (palbociclib vs. ribociclib): the HARMONIA trial (NCT05207709, EudraCT 2021-002027-38) is a Phase III, multicenter, randomised, open-label head-to-head study of ribociclib vs. palbociclib in HR+/HER2-/HER2-Enriched advanced breast cancer, registered on ClinicalTrials.gov, listed on EudraCT, and as of April 2025 still active and not yet recruiting. This is a randomised trial testing the two drugs directly against each other — the sentence says no such trial has ever existed.

**Citation.** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05207709 (HARMONIA trial, SOLTI-2101/AFT-58); EudraCT 2021-002027-38; TiP abstract: ASCO JCO 2023, doi:10.1200/JCO.2023.41.16_suppl.TPS1125; Annals of Oncology 2022 TiP abstract (272TiP); PALMARES-2 results: Annals of Oncology April 2025 (NCT06805812), reported in Targeted Oncology ~August 2026.

**Their words.** “HARMONIA is an international, multicenter, randomized, open-label and phase III study [of] ribociclib vs. palbociclib in patients with advanced HR+/HER2-/HER2-Enriched breast cancer.”

**We inherited this.** The original says: Multiple published network meta-analyses and real-world studies (e.g., Petrelli et al., Breast Cancer Res Treat 2019; Scientific Reports 2024 network meta-analysis) state that there is 'no statistically significant difference in OS between CDK4/6i' based on indirect comparisons of placebo-controlled trials. The original scope of that finding is: indirect/network comparison of phase III trials each vs. placebo, in the metastatic HR+/HER2- setting, for OS as endpoint. The sentence under review drops all those qualifications and converts a scoped indirect-comparison finding into a universal claim that 'every comparison finds nothing between them' — which is false once direct comparative data (PALMARES-2, OPAL registry) and the existence of HARMONIA are considered.

**Does it change the conclusion.** This breaks the sentence, not merely narrows it. The sentence makes two universal claims: (a) no trial has ever tested abemaciclib against either comparator, and (b) every comparison of the two drugs the guideline grades apart finds nothing between them. Claim (b) is broken by HARMONIA, which is a registered, active Phase III RCT directly randomising palbociclib against ribociclib — the sentence says this trial does not exist. Claim (a) is not broken by a completed RCT (no head-to-head RCT of abemaciclib vs. palbociclib or ribociclib has reported results), but the 'nothing between them' assertion is broken by PALMARES-2, which found statistically significant real-world PFS differences between all three agents. Whether this changes the piece's broader conclusion depends on context, but it definitively breaks the sentence as written.

### CE-02 — role says NARROWED (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** Only the category of evidence does.

**What breaks it.** The NCCN's own published definition of the 'Preferred' tier states it is based on 'superior efficacy, safety, and evidence' — meaning evidence quality is a constitutive criterion of the preference designation itself, not a separate system. A Category 1 Preferred regimen (ribociclib+AI) and a Category 2A Preferred regimen (abemaciclib+AI, palbociclib+AI) carry the same preference label but differ in the evidence sub-criterion that defines that label. The claim that 'only the category of evidence does [differ]' is therefore true only in the narrow sense that the preference tier label ('Preferred') is the same word for all three; it is false in the structural sense that the preference designation's own definition makes evidence quality one of its three constitutive components, so the preference designations are not substantively identical across the three drugs.

**Citation.** https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-process/development-and-update-of-guidelines — NCCN.org (official); corroborated by JAMA Network Open 2023 Nov 14;6(11):e2343285 (PMC10646727); and JNCCN v4.2026, doi:10.6004/jnccn.2026.0033

**Their words.** “"Preferred: Interventions that are based on superior efficacy, safety, and evidence; and, when appropriate, affordability."”

**We inherited this.** The original says: The claim appears to be the author's own synthesis of the NCCN guideline structure, not a direct quotation from a guideline or review. The NCCN guideline itself (v4.2026, BINV-P) lists all three AI+CDK4/6i combinations as 'Preferred' first-line options, with ribociclib as Category 1 and abemaciclib/palbociclib as Category 2A. The author's inference — that the preference designation is therefore identical and only the evidence category differs — is a reasonable reading of the label but ignores that the NCCN's definition of 'Preferred' explicitly includes 'evidence' as a sub-criterion, making the two systems partially overlapping rather than fully independent.

**Does it change the conclusion.** This narrows but does not fully reverse the piece's conclusion. The piece argues that the preference label ('Preferred') is the same for all three drugs and that only the evidence category differs — which is literally true at the label level. However, the piece's framing implies the two systems are cleanly independent, when in fact the NCCN's own definition of 'Preferred' explicitly incorporates 'evidence' as one of its three criteria. A reader of the piece would conclude that the preference designation conveys no information about evidence strength, which is incorrect: a Category 2A Preferred regimen is, by NCCN's own definition, one where the evidence component of the preference assessment is weaker. The sentence therefore overstates the independence of the two systems. This matters to the piece's conclusion because the piece uses the shared 'Preferred' label to argue there is no guideline-level ranking among the three drugs — but the evidence-quality difference is embedded in the preference designation's own definition, not merely appended to it in a separate column.

### CE-07 — role says NARROWED (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** The evidence’s answer is that nothing has been shown to separate them: the two randomised trials that have put any pair of them head to head, both ribociclib against palbociclib, found no difference; no randomised trial has compared abemaciclib against either of the others; and none of the four comparative studies on this page can tell abemaciclib and ribociclib apart.

**What breaks it.** The sentence contains three sub-claims. Two survive; one is materially narrowed. (A) 'Both ribociclib against palbociclib' — the MAINTAIN trial (NCT02632045, JCO 2023) is a published, randomised phase II trial that randomised patients to ribociclib vs. placebo after prior CDK4/6i (86.5% of whom had received palbociclib).

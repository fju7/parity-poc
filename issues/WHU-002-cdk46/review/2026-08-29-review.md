I found three publish-blocking findings under the same standards. Two are factual; one is
an internal contradiction about the statistical threshold. I did not find a sourced
problem with the central conclusion that the available comparative evidence has not
established an efficacy difference between abemaciclib and ribociclib.

## Finding 1

**QUOTE**

> "No randomised trial has tested one of these drugs against another. Three research
> programmes have compared them anyway, and reported across four papers."

**BREACH** — Question 2 (what evidence actually supports it) / unsupported factual claim.

**WHY** — There are more than three published comparative research programmes. A 2023
indirect treatment comparison reconstructed patient-level data from PALOMA-2, MONALEESA-2
and MONARCH 3 and directly estimated pairwise differences among all three drugs. A
separate 2023 real-world cohort also compared all three. Both actually reinforce the
article's abemaciclib-versus-ribociclib conclusion, but the statement that only three
programmes have compared them is false.

**SOURCE** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10527344/

> "We aimed to compare overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) between
> three CDK4/6-inhibitors…"

and reports for abemaciclib versus ribociclib an OS HR of 0.933 (95% CI 0.753–1.157,
p = 0.528), with no significant pairwise PFS differences.

A separate real-world comparison (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10217927/)
states that after 42 months:

> "we found no significant differences in OS among the three inhibitors"

and likewise found no statistically significant overall PFS differences among them.

**FIX** — The smallest safe fix is to remove the numerical claim: "No randomised trial has
tested one of these drugs against another. Several research programmes have compared them
indirectly or observationally." The later discussion can still focus on the three studies
the article chose to examine, but it should not present those three as the complete
comparative literature.

## Finding 2

**QUOTE**

> "That it reflects anything about the drugs beyond which trial's survival analysis
> crossed its own significance threshold — on an endpoint none of these trials was
> powered for."

**BREACH** — Question 2 (what evidence actually supports it) / factual error.

**WHY** — PALOMA-2 was explicitly powered for its overall-survival analysis. The
publication says the final OS analysis was planned after at least 390 events and provided
80% power to detect HR ≤0.74 at a one-sided significance level of 0.025. So "none of these
trials was powered for" OS is false.

This is particularly important because MONARCH 3 genuinely was different: its final
publication explicitly says, "No power assumptions were made for the secondary endpoint of
OS." The article is therefore flattening an actual design difference among the trials.

**SOURCE** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10950136/ (PALOMA-2)

> "The final OS analysis was planned to be performed after at least 390 events, providing
> an 80% power to detect a hazard ratio [HR] ≤0.74…"

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S092375342400139X (MONARCH 3)

> "No power assumptions were made for the secondary endpoint of OS."

**FIX** — Delete only the offending clause: "That it reflects anything about the drugs
beyond which trial's survival analysis crossed its own significance threshold." The
surrounding discussion already makes the defensible point that secondary OS analyses can
differ in power and statistical design. There is no need to replace the deleted clause
with another generalization.

## Finding 3

**QUOTE**

> "One interval stopped at 0.93 and the other at 1.02, and that is the whole of it:
> ribociclib's result reached statistical significance (two-sided p = 0.008) and
> abemaciclib's did not (P = .0664). The threshold is the conventional one and the
> guideline applied it correctly."

**BREACH** — Factual error / internal contradiction, and Rule 7's concern with preserving
the actual inferential framework.

**WHY** — The article itself correctly says several paragraphs earlier that MONARCH 3's
final OS result was judged against .034, not .05. The primary publication confirms it:
because of multiplicity control and alpha spending, the final two-sided significance
boundary for the ITT OS analysis was 0.034. Calling "the threshold" conventional in the
paragraph comparing MONALEESA-2 with MONARCH 3 contradicts the statistical framework the
article has just carefully explained.

**SOURCE** — The piece itself says earlier: "MONARCH 3's is two-sided and was judged
against .034, not .05…" The MONARCH 3 publication independently confirms: "the two-sided
P value boundary was 0.034 for the ITT population…"
(https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S092375342400139X)

**FIX** — Delete: "The threshold is the conventional one and the guideline applied it
correctly." The immediately preceding sentence already accurately says which result was
statistically significant and which was not.

## Suspicions I could not source

I would scrutinize the very broad opening statement:

> "Every study that has compared the two it grades apart finds nothing between them…"

My searches found additional comparative studies beyond the three discussed in the
article, including the 2023 indirect comparison above, and those additional studies also
found no statistically significant abemaciclib-versus-ribociclib difference. A 2026
real-world study in patients with bone metastases likewise reports no significant
difference between those two drugs.

So I cannot source a counterexample and therefore am not reporting that sentence as a
finding. But "every study" is an exhaustive claim and the article has already missed
published comparative studies in describing the literature. I would not regard my search
as sufficient to certify the universal statement.

---

Those are my three findings. Importantly, the additional comparative evidence I found
strengthens rather than weakens the article's central abemaciclib-versus-ribociclib
thesis: the problem is that the draft claims an exhaustive literature set that is not
exhaustive, and separately misstates how OS power and significance thresholds worked in
the underlying trials.

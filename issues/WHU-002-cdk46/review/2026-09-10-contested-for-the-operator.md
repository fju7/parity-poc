## G2 — 6 change(s)

### The question

When we replace our paraphrase of a standards body with its verbatim text, and add a scoped negative — *"We asked, on 3 September 2026, and there is no such sentence"* — what exactly is being claimed, and is the search that negative rests on described precisely enough for a reader to repeat it? **A scoped universal negative is only as good as its stated scope**, and this one currently names a date and nothing else: not which version, not which sections, not what was searched for.

### The sentences

> **A defect in HOW the claim is made, independent of what is decided about the
> guideline's text.** *"We asked, on 3 September 2026, and there is no such
> sentence"* names a date and nothing else — not which version of the guideline,
> not which sections, not what string was searched for. **A reader cannot repeat
> it.** Appendix C exists to list universal negatives *so that a reader can try
> to falsify them*, and a negative whose scope is unstated cannot be falsified,
> only believed. That is the Morning Glory class and the RV-09 class in a new
> instance: a search declared conclusive without its bounds stated. Whatever is
> ruled about the verbatim text, that sentence needs its scope — which document,
> which version, which sections, what string.

**1. [changed]**

> *replaced:* Category 1 means high-level evidence and uniform panel consensus.

> *now reads:* The guideline defines category 1 as based upon high-level evidence (≥1 randomized phase 3 trials or high-quality, robust meta-analyses), there is uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% support of the Panel) that the intervention is appropriate , and category 2A as based upon lower-level evidence, there is uniform NCCN consensus (≥85% support of the Panel) that the intervention is appropriate .

**2. [changed]**

> *replaced:* Category 2A means lower-level evidence with uniform consensus.

> *now reads:* The difference between them is the evidence, not the consensus: both require the same ≥85% panel support.

**3. [changed]**

> *replaced:* It is a record of which of its trials produced a significant survival result, and the guideline says so plainly.

> *now reads:* It is a record of which of its trials produced a significant survival result.

**4. [added]**

> *now reads:* That reading is ours, and this is the whole of what the guideline gives for it: it says ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor is category 1 due to the clear overall survival benefit, and that ribociclib and abemaciclib with fulvestrant are category 1 since those combinations showed an overall survival benefit in MONALEESA-3 and MONARCH 2.

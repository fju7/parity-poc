For a QUESTION, from a source only a person is permitted to read:

    ANSWERED BY: a person's name. Not "the team", not a role.
    ON:          the date they opened the document
    ANSWER:      what it says, quoted
    LOCATOR:     section, table or page
    EFFECT:      changes | narrows | sharpens | corrects | none
    DID:         what changed

Or, for a QUESTION the record already answers, a withdrawal:

    WITHDRAWN BY: a person's name, or the role that withdrew it
    ON:           the date
    WHY:          what on the record already settles it
    DID:          what was done instead

by no automated check here, holding the fact that decided the piece.

behaved as though the page had been checked. Where only a person can

## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — SERIOUS / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** A 2025 Campbell systematic review concludes that a range of individual-level interventions work to improve educational outcomes among people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), but that there is little research on systemic or school-level change [1].

**They object.** The page never says that no included study measured attendance, enrolment or completion, so "educational outcomes" in the headline means skills-for-learning measures and nothing about access or retention.

**Their document says.** No study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion of people with disabilities in education.
  — *Results/Main findings section describing outcomes of included studies (after the sentence on outcomes most frequently reflected in reviewed studies)*

**Why it matters.** The page opens by describing the problem as falling behind in enrolment, retention and completion. A reader will assume the interventions that "work" address that problem, but the evidence covers skills outcomes only. The conclusion survives only in this narrower form.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "A 2025 Campbell systematic review concludes that a range of individual-level interventions work to improve educational outcomes among people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), but that there is little research on systemic or school-level change [1].": "The review found that \"no study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion of people with disabilities in education,\" so it offers no evidence on whether these interventions affect those outcomes [1]."

### S001-03 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy were significant, and the review describes all these effects as large but based on a low number of studies [1].

**They object.** The page gives only the "low number of studies" caveat. It omits that the review pairs the literacy effects with very high heterogeneity, possible publication bias and a confidence interval that barely excludes zero.

**Their document says.** Literacy d = 2.46 (0.01 to 4.90) k = 4 n = 389 I ² = 93% Egger's test 3.61 ( t = 1.66, p = 0.24) Large effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially high publication bias
  — *Section 5.3 Effects of interventions, Table 14 Effects of interventions (Literacy row)*

**Why it matters.** A reader will take "significant" and "large" at face value. Estimates this wide and this heterogeneous are better read as direction-of-effect signals than as magnitudes.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "Effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy were significant, and the review describes all these effects as large but based on a low number of studies [1].": "For literacy specifically, the review reports an effect of d = 2.46 (0.01 to 4.90) from 4 studies with 389 participants (I² = 93%), which it describes as a \"Large effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially high publication bias\" [1]."

### S001-05 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Included studies concerned with 'Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education' showed a moderately significant effect [1].

**They object.** The page omits the size, study count and heterogeneity behind "moderately significant". The result rests on four studies with very high heterogeneity and possible publication bias.

**Their document says.** Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education d = 0.35 (0.12 to 0.58) k = 4 n = 401 I² = 86% Egger's test 2.01 ( t = 1.43, p = 0.29) Moderate effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially moderate publication bias
  — *Table 14 Effects of interventions (section 5.3)*

**Why it matters.** This is the page's main positive evidence on school-level change. The review's own caveats show that evidence is weaker than "moderately significant" suggests.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "Included studies concerned with 'Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education' showed a moderately significant effect [1].": "For 'Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education', the review reports an effect of d = 0.35 (0.12 to 0.58) from four studies with 401 participants (I² = 86%), which it summarises as a \"moderate effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially moderate publication bias\" [1]."


### S001-06 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The passages do not identify which interventions work best for different types of disability, although the review set this out as one of its questions [1].

**Their document says.** No studies identified in our review captured education interventions for people with psychosocial/mental impairments.
  — *Results section on participants by impairment type (before Figure 4, 'Participants by impairment type')*

**Why it matters.** The review cannot say which interventions work for which disability, and it also cannot speak to some impairment groups at all. The page should say so.

# Changelog — what changed, and when

This is **not** the correction history. Corrections are in `corrections.md` and
they record where we were wrong. This file records where the evidence moved:
new work appeared, and the assessment changed to match it.

The two are kept apart on purpose. Merged, they would let us bury our own
errors as updates, and would make other people's new findings look like our
mistakes. They run in opposite directions.

Each entry says what appeared, what changed on the page, and against which
version of the page. Every entry is bound to a content hash in `watch.json`,
for the same reason gate acceptances are: an entry unbound from the content it
describes is a claim nobody can check.

## The promise this page makes, and the one it does not

This assessment is **living**: the question is standing, and `watch.json`
holds the questions we are watching and the searches that would answer each.

The page makes the modest promise — we read these documents and this is what
they said, as of a stated date — and no other. It states the date its evidence
was read and claims nothing about the interval since. Until 12 September 2026
it also carried a *Last reviewed* date and an argument for it; that promise
was withdrawn by the operator's ruling of that day
(`review/2026-09-12-modest-promise-ruling.md`), because a review date implies
the page is kept current and we cannot stand behind that: what we watch is
narrower than what could change, and nothing obliges anyone to look before a
reader arrives. The watch runs for us. It is an instrument, not a promise.

## What is being watched

The seven standing questions are in `watch.json` with the searches that would
answer each. In short: whether the randomised crossover trial the study authors
asked for has been run; whether deskilling has been measured in more
specialties; whether the three preprints this piece leans on have been peer
reviewed; whether anything we cite has been corrected or retracted; whether the
mechanism has been measured in live procedures rather than video; whether we
can finally read the commentary we could not; and whether anyone else has
carried this evidence to a general reader with its limitations attached.

# WHU-003 — public correction history

Substantive changes made **after** publication. Draft changes and things caught
in review belong in `review/`, not here — this is the only one of the three
histories a reader sees, and mixing them makes it useless to them. The
changelog (`changelog.md`, `watch.json`) is the other history: it records where
the evidence moved, never where we were wrong.

Each entry: what a reader who saw the earlier version needs to know, and when.
Not how the process failed; that is in the repository.


## 12 September 2026 — we promised a review date, and we are withdrawing the promise

Since 30 August this page's masthead has read <q>Last reviewed 30 August
2026</q>, and a paragraph beneath the assessment argued for it: <q>This page
carries a "last reviewed" date rather than a "last updated" one, and the
difference is deliberate. It is the date somebody last looked, not the date
something last changed. A review that finds nothing is still a review and is
recorded as one.</q> The watch section said <q>the date above is when somebody
last looked</q>, and the publication history said the page <q>carries a
last-reviewed date</q>.

**We are withdrawing the promise, not the argument.** The argument was ours and
we still think it is a good one: when somebody last looked is worth more to a
reader than when something last changed, and a review that finds nothing is
still a review. Neither of those is what a reader takes from a review date on
a page. A review date implies the page is kept current, and we cannot stand
behind that. What we watch is narrower than what could change — we can see a
trial registry move and a paper cite another paper, and we cannot see most of
what happens in a field — and nothing obliges anyone to look before you
arrive. A page trusted more because of a feature that has not run is the
failure this publication exists to point at in other people.

**What the page says now.** The masthead reads <q>Evidence read 30 August
2026</q>. The paragraph that argued for the review date now says what it used
to say and why it no longer says it. The watch section says the date is when
the evidence was read, <q>not a guarantee that anyone has looked since</q>. A
standing note at the head of this history states the bound: <q>As of 30 August
2026 — the date the evidence on this page was read. This page is not monitored
continuously.</q> The publication history no longer lists a last-reviewed date
among what this page carries.

**The watch itself is unchanged.** The same seven standing questions, the same
searches behind them, the same register (`watch.json`, untouched by this
correction). What has changed is that they are our instrument rather than a
promise to you.

The rule behind this is the operator's ruling of 12 September 2026,
`review/2026-09-12-modest-promise-ruling.md`: this publication makes the
modest promise — we read these documents and this is what they said, as of a
stated date — and does not promise currency, because it cannot be sure that
promise is true.

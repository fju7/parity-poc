### CE-01 — role says NARROWED (MEDIUM confidence)

**We say.** No general-audience account has set the Budzyn et al. colonoscopy deskilling result (Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology 2025, adenoma detection falling from 28.4% to 22.4% in non-AI colonoscopies after AI adoption) beside the experimental studies of generative AI and reasoning or essay writing, while also reporting the colonoscopy study's own stated limitations.

**What breaks it.** The PulmCCM newsletter piece 'The New Era of AI De-Skilling is Here' (September 5, 2025) places the Budzyn colonoscopy result directly beside the Kosmyna et al. 'Your Brain on ChatGPT' essay-writing study and the Microsoft/Swiss business-school critical-thinking studies in a single general-audience account. It satisfies two of the three conjunctive conditions. Whether it also reports the colonoscopy study's own stated limitations (observational design, sensitivity to selection bias and confounding — per the paper itself at Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 10:896-903) cannot be confirmed from publicly visible snippets because the full article is subscriber-gated. The ACG EBGI summary (gi.org, September 2025) similarly cites both Budzyn and the Kosmyna essay-writing preprint in the same document, but is a specialist journal summary rather than a clearly general-audience piece. The claim therefore survives only in the narrow form: no confirmed single general-audience account has done all three things simultaneously — the two-out-of-three case (Budzyn + essay-writing AI studies) is clearly broken.

**Citation.** https://www.pulmccm.org/p/the-new-era-of-ai-de-skilling-is (PulmCCM, September 5, 2025); https://gi.org/journals-publications/ebgi/zhou_sep2025/ (ACG EBGI, September 2025); colonoscopy study limitation source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468125325001335 (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 10:896-903, 2025)

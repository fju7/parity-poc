### CE-01 — role says NARROWED (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** No general-audience account has told the non-medical reader whose own work is being reshaped by AI what the medical evidence on deskilling actually shows -- the colonoscopy detection loss, the mammography automation-bias experiments, and the null result at three months -- together with the stated limitations of those studies.

**What breaks it.** The CACM article 'The AI Deskilling Paradox' (November 2025, cacm.acm.org) addresses a general computing/knowledge-worker audience, covers the colonoscopy detection loss, and extends the argument to law, journalism, and software development — but does not appear to cover the mammography automation-bias experiment (Dratsch et al. 2023) or the null result at three months (the Stanford/Gastroenterology pragmatic trial), nor does it state the limitations of the colonoscopy study. The Rad Insights piece 'When AI Reads the Easy Cases' (May 2026) covers both the colonoscopy loss and the Dratsch mammography automation-bias finding together, but is aimed at radiologists, not non-medical workers, and does not cover the null result or study limitations. No single piece found covers all four elements for the non-medical-worker audience.

**Citation.** https://cacm.acm.org/news/the-ai-deskilling-paradox/ ; https://rad-insights.com/ai-deskilling-radiology-training/ ; https://wildetruth.substack.com/p/the-deskilling-cycle

**Their words.** “"Similar issues pop up in law, education, journalism, software development, and other fields." (CACM, Nov 2025)”

**We inherited this.** The original says: The claim appears to be original to the piece being assessed, not inherited from a guideline or prior review. It is a meta-claim about the state of science journalism, not a restatement of a clinical finding.

**Does it change the conclusion.** This changes the sentence but not the piece's conclusion. The CACM article partially breaks the 'colonoscopy' and 'non-medical worker' prongs of the claim, and the Rad Insights piece partially breaks the 'colonoscopy + mammography' prong — but no single account assembles all four required elements (colonoscopy loss + mammography automation-bias + null result at three months + stated limitations) for the non-medical-worker audience. The claim survives in its full conjunctive form but is demonstrably narrowed: the colonoscopy finding alone has reached general-audience computing coverage, and the colonoscopy + mammography pairing has reached specialist-adjacent coverage. The claim would need to be rewritten to specify that the full four-element synthesis has not appeared, rather than asserting that none of the individual elements has.

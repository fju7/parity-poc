I found two publish-blocking findings. I did not find a rule-11 problem with the coverage discussion in the searches I ran, and the piece otherwise answers the four required questions unusually explicitly. My review is of the Issue 3 page you supplied.

## Finding 1

**QUOTE**

> "So the honest position on a null result is that we do not have one."

**BREACH** — Question 3 — What evidence weakens or qualifies the claim? Also a factual error as written.

**WHY** — A directly relevant prospective multicentre colonoscopy study was published online on 3 June 2026, nearly three months before this assessment. It exposed endoscopists to computer-aided detection, subsequently removed it, and found no evidence of deskilling in unassisted colonoscopy. It is not the randomized crossover trial the page wants, and its endpoint was PDR-5 rather than adenoma detection rate, but it is plainly a null result on the central question and materially qualifies the Polish retrospective result.

The study is especially difficult to omit because its design is much closer to the causal sequence the article says researchers need: before CADe → during CADe → after CADe removal. The page can explain why it does not settle the matter, but it cannot publish the statement that no null result exists.

**SOURCE** — Pedersen et al., Endoscopy, 2026 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42235541/

The authors conclude: "In non-CADe-assisted colonoscopy, no upskilling or deskilling was observed following a period of CADe exposure."

The study was prospective and multicentre, involved 13 endoscopists and 5,013 colonoscopies, and compared performance before exposure, during CADe use, and after removal.

**FIX** — Remove the sentence claiming there is no null result and add the Pedersen study to the balance of evidence. The smallest defensible addition would say that a 2026 prospective multicentre study found no change in unassisted PDR-5 after CADe exposure, while noting that it used PDR-5 rather than ADR and was not randomized.

This also requires revising the later statement: "Every measurement here is three months or less, and none of them followed anyone for months and found no decay…" because the page's evidence survey is presently incomplete once Pedersen is included.

## Finding 2

**QUOTE**

> "We therefore do not know what it corrects or whether it touches any figure on this page."

**BREACH** — Factual/support error; it also weakens Question 3 because the piece explicitly leaves uncertainty hanging over its principal study that can now be resolved.

**WHY** — The Lancet correction is accessible and states what was corrected. A covariate — indication for colonoscopy — had been omitted from the multivariable analysis in supplementary table 6. The journal says the resulting changes affected two variables and did not affect interpretation of the data.

Nothing in the correction indicates that the headline 28.4% versus 22.4% unadjusted ADR figures used by this article were changed. Leaving the correction described as unknown therefore creates uncertainty that the available source resolves.

**SOURCE** — Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology correction, November 2025 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468125325002948

The correction states: "A covariate (indication for colonoscopy) had been mistakenly omitted from the multivariable analysis presented in supplementary table 6…" It further says the changes do not affect interpretation.

**FIX** — Replace the unread-correction paragraph with the smallest sourced correction, for example: the correction added the omitted colonoscopy-indication covariate to supplementary table 6; it affected two variables in that analysis, and the journal says the changes do not alter interpretation. Remove the request asking readers to obtain the correction.

The Pedersen paper changes the evidentiary landscape of the article: it does not overturn the Polish result, but Issue 3 currently tells readers that the relevant contrary prospective evidence does not exist when, as of August 30, 2026, it does.

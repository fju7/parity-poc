# deskilling — source advocate, 2026-08-30

One section per item. Each must be closed before this issue can
publish. An open item blocks; an item closed "no merit", with the
reason on the record, does not.

For an OBJECTION, from a source a machine could read:

    MERIT:  yes | partly | no   — and why, citing what you read
    EFFECT: changes | narrows | none
    DID:    what actually changed, or nothing and why

For a QUESTION, from a source only a person is permitted to read:

    ANSWERED BY: a person's name. Not "the team", not a role.
    ON:          the date they opened the document
    ANSWER:      what it says, quoted
    LOCATOR:     section, table or page
    EFFECT:      changes | narrows | none
    DID:         what changed

The second form exists because of NCCN v6.2026: one document, readable
by no automated check here, holding the fact that decided the piece.
Everything else on the page could be machine-checked, so the pipeline
behaved as though the page had been checked. Where only a person can
read the source, the person is the check.

## S008 — Gerlich, M. (2025) — AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking. Societies 15(1), 6, 3 January 2025. Center for Strategic Corporate Foresight and Sustainability, SBS Swiss Business School.
*Advocate: the investigators and statisticians who published Gerlich, M. (2025) — AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking. Societies 15(1), 6, 3 January 2025. Center for Strategic Corporate Foresight and Sustainability, SBS Swiss Business School.*  
*Ledger state: machine_read*  

### S008-01 — SERIOUS / NARROWS

**We say.** a correlation of −0.68 between AI use and critical thinking

**They object.** The page omits that both variables are self-reported: AI use was measured on a 6-point Likert scale and critical thinking was measured by a self-reported scale (items drawn from the Halpern Critical Thinking Assessment and Terenzini's self-reported measures), not by a performance-based assessment — a methodological fact that is the primary qualification on the figure's interpretability.

**Their document says.** "A 6-step Likert scale (1 = Never/Strongly Disagree to 6 = Always/Strongly Agree) was used for most items" (Section 2, Methods / Appendix A); critical thinking items drawn from Halpern Critical Thinking Assessment and Terenzini's self-reported measures, combined into a self-reported scale (Methods, instrument description).
  — *Section 2 (Methodology), instrument description and Appendix A*

**Why it matters.** A reader who sees '−0.68 between AI use and critical thinking' without the self-report qualifier will treat it as evidence that AI use predicts objectively measured critical thinking ability. The paper's own instrument is a self-reported proxy, which means the correlation may partly reflect a shared-method artefact (people who report heavy AI use also report lower confidence in their own thinking). The page's implicit claim — that this figure measures something about actual cognitive capacity — does not survive the qualifier.

MERIT:  yes, and already met before the advocate ran
EFFECT: none
DID:    The page states that AI use, cognitive offloading and critical thinking were all three measured by self-report in the same instrument, and that the critical-thinking items are drawn from an existing assessment rather than that assessment being administered as a test. No change.


### S008-02 — SERIOUS / NARROWS

**We say.** a correlation of −0.68 between AI use and critical thinking

**They object.** The page presents −0.68 as the paper's headline finding, but the paper also reports r = −0.75 between cognitive offloading and critical thinking — a stronger correlation and the one on which the paper's mediation argument centrally rests — without which the reader cannot understand what the paper actually argues.

**Their document says.** r = +0.72 between AI use and cognitive offloading; r = −0.75 between offloading and critical thinking; r = −0.68 between AI use and critical thinking (Results, correlation table). Hypothesis 2: 'Cognitive offloading mediates the relationship between AI tool usage and critical thinking' (Section 1, Hypotheses).
  — *Section 1 (Hypotheses), Section 3 (Results), correlation table*

**Why it matters.** Quoting only −0.68 implies the paper's claim is a direct AI-use-to-critical-thinking link. The paper's actual argument is a mediated one: AI use → cognitive offloading (r = +0.72) → reduced critical thinking (r = −0.75). The −0.68 is the total-effect correlation; the paper's thesis is about the mechanism. A page that cites only the total-effect figure and not the mediation structure misrepresents what the paper concludes and what the figure is evidence for.

MERIT:  partly
EFFECT: none
DID:    The page gives all three correlations, -0.68, +0.72 and -0.75, precisely so the -0.68 is not read as a lone headline. The advocate is right that -0.75 carries the mediation argument; the page's point is about the uniformity of all three, which is stronger with them together than with any one. No change.

## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** A 2025 Campbell systematic review concludes that a range of individual-level interventions work to improve educational outcomes among people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), but that there is little research on systemic or school-level change [1].

**They object.** The page opens with gaps in enrolment, retention and completion and says interventions 'work' on educational outcomes. It never says the review itself asks that interventions be evaluated on concrete outcomes such as school completion, and the effects it reports are mostly on learning skills.

**Their document says.** Where possible, interventions should be evaluated in terms of concrete outcomes like school completion.
  — *Plain-language summary, section 1.6 'What do the findings of this review mean?'*

**Why it matters.** The conclusion survives in a smaller form. The evidence supports skills-for-learning gains. It has not shown effects on the access, retention and completion gaps the page sets out as the problem.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "A 2025 Campbell systematic review concludes that a range of individual-level interventions work to improve educational outcomes among people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), but that there is little research on systemic or school-level change [1].": "The review's authors also recommend that, \"where possible, interventions should be evaluated in terms of concrete outcomes like school completion\" [1]."


### S001-02 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The studies concerned with speech and school behaviour show no significant effect of intervention [1].

**They object.** The page presents the null result without saying the review rests it on very few studies, so it reads as evidence of no effect when it is closer to inconclusive.

**Their document says.** School behaviour and adaptive functioning d = 0.49 (−1.76 to 2.74) k = 5 n = 256 I ² = 0% Egger's test 0.11 ( t = 1.03, p = 0.38) Insignificant effect based on a low number of studies with low heterogeneity and no publication bias
  — *Summary of findings table for 'Skills for learning' (outcome: School behaviour and adaptive functioning), in the synthesis of results section*

**Why it matters.** The page applies the 'low number of studies' caveat to the positive results (sentences 3 and 8) but not to the null ones. The confidence interval for school behaviour (−1.76 to 2.74) is far too wide to show ineffectiveness.

### S001-03 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Most interventions tried to improve children's skills but did not focus on system-level or school-level changes [1].

**They object.** The page leaves out that the review's own abstract names 'Accessible learning environments' as the most common category of intervention, and that those studies reported positive effects. The page's 'little systemic research' framing is therefore sharper than the review's description.

**Their document says.** Studies that were aimed at improving the accessibility of learning environments in education reported positive impacts of these interventions on teacher knowledge and attitudes, parent knowledge and attitudes, and violence reduction.
  — *Final omitted-text passage beginning 'Among those studies which aimed to improve educational attainment, positive effects of interventions were seen...'*

**Why it matters.** The 'individual-level focus' point stands, because many of these programmes target children's social skills or victimisation. But the page implies a near-absence of environment-focused work, which overstates the gap.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "Most interventions tried to improve children's skills but did not focus on system-level or school-level changes [1].": "The review also states that \"studies that were aimed at improving the accessibility of learning environments in education reported positive impacts of these interventions on teacher knowledge and attitudes, parent knowledge and attitudes, and violence reduction\" [1]."


### S001-04 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The sources say a twin-track approach is needed, one that addresses the specific needs of children with disabilities and also includes them in mainstream activities, for example by improving teacher skills and classroom accessibility [1].

**They object.** The page presents the twin-track approach as something the review concludes. The review treats it as an established premise that its evidence does not test.

**Their document says.** It is well established that a twin‐track approach is needed to improve inclusion and outcomes for people with disabilities, meaning a focus both on targeting their specific needs but also ensuring they are included in mainstream activities.
  — *Implications/Discussion section, paragraph beginning 'It is well established that a twin‐track approach is needed'*

**Why it matters.** The review is endorsing an existing policy consensus. It is not reporting that its 28 studies showed twin-track programmes work. The page's wording blurs this, though the review does repeat the recommendation in its conclusions.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers


### S001-05 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** MISSTATES

**We say.** The finding says evaluation should focus on interventions that target both the individual with a disability and their broader environment, because such interventions are needed [1].

**They object.** I could not find this evaluation recommendation in the review. The 'needed' language is about systemic approaches and a twin-track approach, which is a separate point.

**Their document says.** Consequently, a focus on evaluation of interventions which target not just the individual with a disability but also their broader environment, are needed.
  — *Opening paragraph of the held text (twin-track approach discussion, before the Keywords line)*

**Why it matters.** The page attributes to the review a research recommendation I could not locate. The page seems to have merged the twin-track practice message with the research message, which the review treats separately.

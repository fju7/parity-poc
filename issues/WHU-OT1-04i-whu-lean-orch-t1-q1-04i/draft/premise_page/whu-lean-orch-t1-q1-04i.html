What part can carbon dioxide removal play in reaching net zero?

The question

The question is: “Alongside cutting emissions, what part can removing carbon dioxide from the air play in reaching net zero, now and in the decades ahead?”

The premise

This question rested on a premise: that the primary sources would show a material gap, overstatement or understatement between them and the belief we recorded before reading them.

The belief we recorded: “Removing CO2 from the air can't replace cutting emissions.”

The belief we recorded: “Today almost all removal comes from trees and forests, and machines like direct air capture remove a trivial amount at high cost.”

The belief we recorded: “To reach net zero we'll still need a lot more removal later this century to cancel out leftover emissions from things like aviation, farming and cement, but it's risky to count on technologies that don't exist at scale yet.”

What the sources we hold show instead

The report states plainly that, within ambitious mitigation strategies at global or national levels, CDR cannot serve as a substitute for deep emissions reductions but can fulfil multiple complementary roles [2]. Modelled pathways reaching net zero include several elements: transitioning from fossil fuels without carbon capture and storage to very low- or zero-carbon energy sources, demand-side measures, improving efficiency, reducing non-CO2 emissions, and CDR [1]. Some hard-to-abate residual emissions remain, for example some emissions from agriculture, aviation, shipping and industrial processes [1]. These residual emissions would need to be counterbalanced by CDR to achieve net zero CO2 or GHG emissions (high confidence) [1]. Net zero CO2 is reached earlier than net zero GHGs (high confidence) [1, 2].

What we conclude

The evidence we hold does not support this premise.

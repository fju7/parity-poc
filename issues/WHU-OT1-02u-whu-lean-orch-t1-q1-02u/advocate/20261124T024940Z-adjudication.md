## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The clearest reported effects are for interventions that build learners' skills, with significant and large effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy, but these effects are based on a low number of studies [1].

**They object.** The page says only that these effects rest on few studies. It leaves out that the review's own table gives very wide confidence intervals for these estimates and flags them as high-heterogeneity or small-sample effects, which makes 'clearest' too strong.

**Their document says.** Numeracy d = 4.60 (0.69 to 8.50) k = 2 n = 110 I ² = 85% Egger's test Low sample size High effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity.
  — *Section 5.3 Effects of interventions, Table 14 (Effects of interventions), Skills for learning, Numeracy row*

**Why it matters.** The direction of effect holds, but 'clearest' overstates it. The magnitudes are imprecise and the review itself says the pooled estimate carries considerable heterogeneity. The page should say the size of these effects is highly uncertain, not just that few studies contributed.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers


### S001-02 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** It holds up that a range of individual-level interventions, especially those building skills such as literacy and numeracy, have shown significant effects, though the authors call the support 'emerging' and the effects rest on few studies [1].

**They object.** The page names literacy and numeracy as the robust core. It omits the review's outcome-level caveats: the numeracy estimate has I² of 85% and a confidence interval of 0.69 to 8.50, and the review treats publication bias for some of these outcomes as likely or unassessable.

**Their document says.** Numeracy d = 4.60 (0.69 to 8.50) k = 2 n = 110 I ² = 85% Egger's test Low sample size High effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity.
  — *Section 5.3 Effects of interventions, Table 14 (Skills for learning, Numeracy row)*

**Why it matters.** The conclusion that skills interventions show significant effects survives. The page's 'it holds up' framing is narrower than the review supports, because the headline outcomes are the ones with the least precise and potentially inflated estimates.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers


### S001-03 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The review set out to ask which interventions appear most effective for different types of disability, but the passages used here do not report an answer to that question [1].

**They object.** The page is right that no answer is reported, but the review does report why one is hard to give. Its evidence by impairment type is lopsided, and it found no education intervention studies at all for psychosocial or mental impairments.

**Their document says.** Individuals with intellectual or learning and developmental impairments were the most frequently targeted by included interventions ( n = 16). This was followed by individuals with hearing impairments ( n = 7), physical impairments ( n = 4), and then vision impairments ( n = 2). No studies identified in our review captured education interventions for people with psychosocial/mental impairments.
  — *Section 5.1 Target group (results text on impairment type, preceding Figure 4)*

**Why it matters.** The page's statement stands. The omitted distribution (vision n=2, psychosocial none) explains the gap and tells readers where the evidence is thinnest, which is more useful than a bare statement that no answer was found.

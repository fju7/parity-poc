    MERIT:  yes | partly | no   — and why, citing what you read
            (or not_ruled_protected, only on a protected sentence)
    EFFECT: changes | narrows | sharpens | corrects | none

    OMISSION: add | out_of_scope | not_material | not_in_source | not_added_cap
    DID:      what was added, or why nothing was

For a QUESTION, from a source only a person is permitted to read:

    ANSWERED BY: a person's name. Not "the team", not a role.
    ON:          the date they opened the document
    ANSWER:      what it says, quoted
    LOCATOR:     section, table or page
    EFFECT:      changes | narrows | sharpens | corrects | none
    DID:         what changed

Or, for a QUESTION the record already answers, a withdrawal:

    WITHDRAWN BY: a person's name, or the role that withdrew it
    ON:           the date
    WHY:          what on the record already settles it
    DID:          what was done instead

The second form exists because of NCCN v6.2026: one document, readable
by no automated check here, holding the fact that decided the piece.
Everything else on the page could be machine-checked, so the pipeline
behaved as though the page had been checked. Where only a person can
read the source, the person is the check.

## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — SERIOUS / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The review covers people with disabilities generally rather than only children and young people [1].

**They object.** The page implies a broad, all-ages evidence base, but the review reports that almost all included studies targeted children, so the findings are really about children.

**Their document says.** Children with disabilities fall behind in educational outcomes as the current school systems are not set up to teach children with different impairment types.
  — *Authors' Conclusions (abstract and main Authors' conclusions section)*

**Why it matters.** The review's scope covers all people with disabilities, but 25 of its 28 studies were with children. Without that figure, a reader would assume the effects apply to adults and young people, which the review's evidence does not support.

OMISSION: out_of_scope
DID:      nothing added; out of scope (How many disabled children are out of school, and why); listed for readers


### S001-02 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** A 2025 Campbell systematic review concludes that a range of individual-level interventions work to improve educational outcomes among people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), but there is little research on systemic or school-level change [1].

**They object.** The lead sentence gives the review's headline claim without the authors' explicit instruction, stated in the abstract and summary, to interpret the findings cautiously.

**Their document says.** "However, the findings of this review must be interpreted cautiously, given the low confidence in study methods, severe heterogeneity and significant publication bias."
  — *Plain language summary / abstract (main findings)*

**Why it matters.** The review's own caveat uses stronger wording ("severe" heterogeneity, "significant" publication bias) than the page's later "high" and "frequently high". That caveat belongs next to the word "work" in the opening sentence, not several sentences later.

OMISSION: not_in_source
DID:      nothing added: the quotation is not in the held text of S001


### S001-03 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The interventions and outcome measures differed across studies, which made direct comparison difficult, so the review does not establish a single best kind of programme [1].

**They object.** The page treats the lack of a best programme as a measurement limitation, but the authors state it as a substantive conclusion.

**Their document says.** There is no one ‘magic bullet’ intervention which can equalise health outcomes for this group.
  — *Authors' Conclusions (summary) and AUTHORS' CONCLUSIONS section*

**Why it matters.** The page's conclusion stands. However, the authors say no single intervention suffices and draw the twin-track recommendation from that. The page reads the same point as only "we can't compare".

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers


### S001-04 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Because interventions and outcome measures varied so much, the sources do not support ranking one kind of programme as the best [1].

**They object.** The page again attributes the lack of a ranking only to heterogeneity, and omits the authors' own statement that no single intervention is the answer.

**Their document says.** "There is no one ‘magic bullet’ intervention which can equalise health outcomes for this group."
  — *Authors' Conclusions (abstract)*

**Why it matters.** This is the same omission as above, repeated in the closing summary. The authors make an affirmative claim, not merely a statement that the evidence can't rank programmes.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers


### S001-05 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** MISSTATES

**We say.** Programmes that change schools, systems or institutions have been studied far less, so the evidence is too thin to say whether they work [1, 2].

**They object.** The review does report some effects for inclusion-focused and teacher-focused interventions, so the evidence is thin but not silent.

**Their document says.** "included studies concerned with ‘Conditions for inclusion of people with disabilities in education’ showed a moderately significant effect, and one study concerned with teacher knowledge showed a significant effect size."
  — *Abstract / Main results (intervention effects)*

**Why it matters.** The page itself cites these results earlier (the 'Conditions for inclusion' and teacher-knowledge sentences), then concludes the evidence cannot say whether system-level programmes work. The page should say the evidence is sparse and low-confidence, with limited positive signals, rather than that it cannot say anything.

MERIT:  yes — The source says there is 'little research on systemic or school-level change' and calls the absence of evidence 'a very striking gap', but it also reports that studies on 'Conditions for inclusion' 'showed a moderately significant effect' and 'one study concerned with teacher knowledge showed a significant effect size', so the evidence is thin rather than unable to say anything.
EFFECT: corrects
DID:    the sentence is to be replaced word for word with: "Programmes that change schools, systems or institutions have been studied far less, and the few studies, on conditions for inclusion and on teacher knowledge, showed some positive effects, but the evidence remains thin [1, 2]."; ruled by ruling model claude-opus-5-5, prompt 388a27cee450

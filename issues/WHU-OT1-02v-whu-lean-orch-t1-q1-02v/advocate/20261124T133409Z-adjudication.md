One section per item. Each must be closed before this issue can
publish. An open item blocks; an item closed "no merit", with the
reason on the record, does not.

For an OBJECTION, from a source a machine could read:

    MERIT:  yes | partly | no   — and why, citing what you read
            (or not_ruled_protected, only on a protected sentence)
    EFFECT: changes | narrows | sharpens | corrects | none
    DID:    what actually changed, or nothing and why

For an OMISSION (type LEAVES_OUT), closed by the omission ruling:

    OMISSION: add | out_of_scope | not_material | not_in_source | not_added_cap
    DID:      what was added, or why nothing was

For a QUESTION, from a source only a person is permitted to read:

    ANSWERED BY: a person's name. Not "the team", not a role.
    ON:          the date they opened the document
    ANSWER:      what it says, quoted
    LOCATOR:     section, table or page
    EFFECT:      changes | narrows | sharpens | corrects | none
    DID:         what changed

Or, for a QUESTION the record already answers, a withdrawal:

    WITHDRAWN BY: a person's name, or the role that withdrew it
    ON:           the date
    WHY:          what on the record already settles it
    DID:          what was done instead

The second form exists because of NCCN v6.2026: one document, readable
by no automated check here, holding the fact that decided the piece.
Everything else on the page could be machine-checked, so the pipeline
behaved as though the page had been checked. Where only a person can
read the source, the person is the check.

## S001 — Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.
*Advocate: the authors of Effectiveness of interventions for improving educational outcomes for people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries: A systematic review.*  
*Ledger state: full_text_held*  

### S001-01 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** A 2025 Campbell systematic review concludes that a range of individual-level interventions work to improve educational outcomes among people with disabilities in low- and middle-income countries, but that there is little research on systemic or school-level change [1].

**They object.** The page states 'work' flatly. The authors attach an explicit instruction to read their findings cautiously, and this does not appear next to the claim.

**Their document says.** "However, the findings of this review must be interpreted cautiously, given the low confidence in study methods, severe heterogeneity and significant publication bias." (Discussion/Summary of main results, as it appears in the full-text PDF). The abstract's Main Results also say: "Across studies, heterogeneity is high, and risk of publication bias varies but was frequently high."
  — *Discussion (summary of main results); Abstract, Main Results*

**Why it matters.** The page does mention low confidence and bias in later sentences. But the headline claim that interventions 'work' is not tied to the authors' own warning that the findings are inflated by weak design, heterogeneity and publication bias. A reader of the first sentence alone gets a stronger result than the authors claim.

OMISSION: not_in_source
DID:      nothing added: the quotation is not in the held text of S001


### S001-02 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** Interventions aimed at skills for learning showed significant effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy, and the review calls these effects large but based on a low number of studies [1].

**They object.** The page omits the pooled estimates and their confidence intervals. These show the 'large' effects are very imprecise, and for literacy the lower bound is almost zero. The review also flags potentially high publication bias.

**Their document says.** Literacy d = 2.46 (0.01 to 4.90) k = 4 n = 389 I ² = 93% Egger's test 3.61 ( t = 1.66, p = 0.24) Large effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially high publication bias
  — *5.3 Effects of interventions, Table 14 (Effects of interventions), Skills for learning, Literacy row*

**Why it matters.** The page says 'significant' and 'large' and notes the low number of studies. It does not say the effects come from k = 2 to 4 studies per outcome with I² of 85–93%, and with intervals running from near zero to implausibly large. The direction is supported, but the size is not.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "Interventions aimed at skills for learning showed significant effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy, and the review calls these effects large but based on a low number of studies [1].": "For literacy specifically, the review reports d = 2.46 (0.01 to 4.90) from 4 studies with 389 participants and I² = 93%, which it describes as a \"Large effect based on a low number of studies with high heterogeneity and potentially high publication bias\" [1]."


### S001-03 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The effects on literacy, cognitive skills, handwriting and numeracy were significant, and the review calls all of them large but based on a low number of studies [1].

### S001-04 — MINOR / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** According to the source, all but one study received an overall rating of low confidence in study findings [1].

**They object.** The page gives the low-confidence rating but not the study-design makeup behind it. Only four of the 28 included studies were randomised trials, and that is the evidence for 'works'.

**Their document says.** The four high confidence ratings were given for RCTs.
  — *Results, design ratings for confidence in study findings (paragraph following the list of studies rated low on design)*

**Why it matters.** It matters to the 'work' conclusion that 24 of 28 studies were non-randomised designs. The page lists one RCT and one uncontrolled before-and-after study as examples of the range, but never gives the proportion.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers


### S001-05 — SERIOUS / NARROWS

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** They also recommend a focus on interventions that address attendance, enrolment, completion and transition to higher levels of education, not only individual skills [1].

**They object.** The page gives the recommendation but not the finding behind it. No included study measured attendance, enrolment or completion. This is the evidence gap that explains the recommendation.

**Their document says.** "No study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion of people with disabilities in education."
  — *Results – outcome categories table (Attendance, enrolment, and completion); Authors' Conclusions 7.1*

**Why it matters.** The review's headline outcomes are access and persistence in school, which are the gaps named in its own background. The page says no studies covered two intervention categories. It does not say that no studies measured the outcomes of attendance, enrolment and completion, so the evidence on 'educational outcomes' is narrower than the page implies. 'Work' applies only to skills and attainment measures and conditions for inclusion.

OMISSION: add
DID:      to be inserted word for word after "They also recommend a focus on interventions that address attendance, enrolment, completion and transition to higher levels of education, not only individual skills [1].": "The review reports that \"no study looked at outcomes related to attendance, enrolment, and completion of people with disabilities in education\" [1]."


### S001-06 — MINOR / NONE

**Type.** LEAVES_OUT

**Nearest page sentence.** The authors call for studies with larger samples, more rigorous designs and standardised measures [1].

**They object.** The page omits the authors' diagnosis of why Children with disabilities fall behind: school systems are not set up to teach them. This is the premise of the twin-track recommendation.

**Their document says.** "Children with disabilities fall behind in educational outcomes as the current school systems are not set up to teach children with different impairment types."
  — *Abstract, Authors' Conclusions*

**Why it matters.** The page reports the twin-track recommendation without the authors' stated reason. The reason supports the point that individual-level interventions alone cannot close the gap. The conclusion stands, but its rationale is missing.

OMISSION: not_material
DID:      nothing added; not material; listed for readers

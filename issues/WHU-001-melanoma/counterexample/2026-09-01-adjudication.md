### CE-01 — role says NARROWED (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** Adjuvant melanoma trials have a history of recurrence benefits that took years to translate into survival benefits, and sometimes never did — for adjuvant PD-1 inhibitors specifically, the comparison arm in this very trial, no randomised trial against placebo has reported a statistically significant overall survival benefit as a prespecified endpoint in its own population, though indirect comparisons have suggested a signal.

**What breaks it.** Ipilimumab (a CTLA-4 inhibitor, not a PD-1 inhibitor) showed a statistically significant OS benefit vs. placebo as a prespecified endpoint in EORTC 18071 (HR 0.72, p=0.001 at 5.3-year follow-up), establishing that adjuvant checkpoint inhibitor therapy against placebo CAN show a prespecified OS benefit — but ipilimumab is not a PD-1 inhibitor. The claim's PD-1 scoping is therefore load-bearing and must be stated explicitly. For PD-1 inhibitors vs. placebo specifically, the claim survives: KEYNOTE-054's OS is a secondary endpoint not yet reported (planned after 380 deaths or ~10 years), and CheckMate 238 compared nivolumab vs. ipilimumab, not placebo. A Clinical Cancer Research paper (2023) confirms: 'Ipilimumab is the only adjuvant therapy that has been shown to provide a statistically significant increase in overall survival (OS) compared with placebo using prespecified criteria.' The claim is true for PD-1 inhibitors vs. placebo but is written in a context ('the comparison arm in this very trial') that implies PD-1 is the relevant class — that scoping must be made explicit or the sentence misleads by omission of the ipilimumab OS result.

**Citation.** Eggermont AM et al., N Engl J Med 2016 (EORTC 18071 OS update): PMID 27717298; Weber J et al. / Ascierto PA et al., CheckMate 238, NCT02388906; Ascierto PA et al., Clin Cancer Res 2023, https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/29/17/3352/728540/

**Their words.** “"Ipilimumab is the only adjuvant therapy that has been shown to provide a statistically significant increase in overall survival (OS) compared with placebo using prespecified criteria"”

**We inherited this.** The original says: The Clinical Cancer Research 2023 paper on CheckMate 238 5-year results states the narrower, accurate version: 'Ipilimumab is the only adjuvant therapy that has been shown to provide a statistically significant increase in overall survival (OS) compared with placebo using prespecified criteria; however, the CheckMate 238 study established the superiority of nivolumab versus ipilimumab for recurrence-free survival.' The original wording is scoped to 'adjuvant therapy' broadly and explicitly carves out ipilimumab. Our sentence drops the ipilimumab carve-out.

**Does it change the conclusion.** The sentence's conclusion — that no adjuvant checkpoint inhibitor vs. placebo has shown a prespecified OS benefit — is false for the class as a whole (ipilimumab broke it in 2016). The sentence is only defensible if 'PD-1 inhibitors specifically' is understood to exclude CTLA-4 inhibitors throughout. Because the sentence opens with 'adjuvant melanoma trials have a history…' and then narrows to PD-1, a reader could reasonably take the broader framing as the claim. The narrowing to PD-1 vs. placebo is correct and should be preserved, but the sentence needs to explicitly acknowledge that ipilimumab (CTLA-4) vs. placebo DID show a prespecified OS benefit, or it creates a false impression about the broader class.

**Searched.** PubMed/literature: 'adjuvant PD-1 inhibitor melanoma overall survival randomised trial placebo'; 'adjuvant melanoma PD-1 pembrolizumab nivolumab overall survival benefit statistically significant'; 'KEYNOTE-054 overall survival final results 2024 2025'; 'CheckMate 238 nivolumab overall survival final analysis melanoma adjuvant'; 'ipilimumab adjuvant melanoma EORTC 18071 overall survival statistically significant placebo'; 'adjuvant melanoma PD-1 overall survival prespecified endpoint statistically significant 2024 2025'. ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT02362594 (KEYNOTE-054) — OS listed as secondary endpoint, NOT_POSTED, anticipated posting 2026-11. NCT02388906 (CheckMate 238) — comparator is ipilimumab, not placebo. ESMO 2024 abstract 1095P (KEYNOTE-054 7-year update): OS analysis planned after 380 deaths. Annals of Oncology 2025 (CheckMate 238 9-year final): OS HR 0.88, not statistically significant vs. ipilimumab. EudraCT 2014-004944-37 (KEYNOTE-054). ### CE-08 — role says NARROWED (HIGH confidence)

**We say.** We had said no adjuvant PD-1 trial has shown a survival benefit, which is too absolute — no randomised trial against placebo has done so as a prespecified endpoint, but indirect comparisons have suggested a signal.

**What breaks it.** The self-correction itself is not fully accurate. CheckMate 238 (nivolumab vs. ipilimumab) is not a placebo-controlled trial, so it correctly falls outside the corrected claim. However, the corrected claim ('no randomised trial against placebo has done so as a prespecified endpoint') omits that OS in KEYNOTE-054 is a SECONDARY (not exploratory) endpoint that has simply not yet been reported — it is planned after 380 deaths or 10 years (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02362594). The corrected sentence implies the endpoint has been tested and failed; in fact it has not yet been tested. Additionally, the 9-year CheckMate 238 final analysis (ESMO 2025) showed OS HR=0.88 (95% CI 0.69–1.11), not statistically significant, confirming no OS benefit vs. ipilimumab either. The self-correction is directionally right but imprecise: the correct statement is that no adjuvant PD-1 vs. placebo trial has YET REPORTED OS as a prespecified endpoint (not that it has been tested and found non-significant).

**Citation.** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02362594 (KEYNOTE-054): OS secondary endpoint, NOT_POSTED, anticipated 2026-11. Annals of Oncology 2025, CheckMate 238 9-year final: https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)03157-6/fulltext. ASCO Post Oct 2025: https://ascopost.com/news/october-2025/nivolumab-vs-ipilimumab-in-resected-stage-iii-or-iv-melanoma-9-year-follow-up-of-checkmate-238/

**Their words.** “"The overall survival analysis is planned after 380 deaths or 10 years from the randomization of the last patient, whichever occurs first."”

**We inherited this.** The original says: The original overclaim ('no adjuvant PD-1 trial has shown a survival benefit') appears to derive from ASCO Post 2021 commentary on KEYNOTE-054: 'Thus far, no trial has reported survival data for adjuvant anti–PD-1 antibody treatment in patients with stage III melanoma.' That 2021 statement was accurate at the time and scoped to 'reported survival data' — it was not a claim that OS benefit was absent, only that it had not yet been reported. The piece appears to have hardened 'not yet reported' into 'has not shown,' which is the error the self-correction is trying to fix.

**Does it change the conclusion.** The self-correction changes the piece's conclusion from 'no PD-1 trial has shown OS benefit' (too absolute, ignores indirect comparisons) to 'no placebo-controlled PD-1 trial has shown OS as a prespecified endpoint' (more accurate but still imprecise). The further precision needed is: KEYNOTE-054's OS has not been reported yet (not that it failed), and the indirect signal comes from the ipilimumab EORTC 18071 OS result plus network meta-analyses. The self-correction is an improvement but still slightly misleads by implying the endpoint was tested rather than pending.

Finding 1

QUOTE
"When the same data were published in The Lancet, they came with two-sided p = 0.053."

BREACH
Rule 7 — State the inferential framework before quoting a p-value.

WHY
The piece correctly explains in the surrounding sentences that the trial used a prespecified one-sided framework, but your rule is explicit: when giving the two-sided p-value, that fact must be stated in the same sentence. Here it is not. The underlying statistical discussion is otherwise accurate.

SOURCE
The ASCO presentation describing the KEYNOTE-942 design states: "Designed with 80% power to detect a hazard ratio of 0.5 with 40 RFS events (with a 1-sided alpha of 0.1 per protocol)."
The peer-reviewed JCO update also confirms the relevant three-year framework and results, while the original Lancet publication reports the primary analysis.
[ASCO KEYNOTE-942 study-design presentation](https://s29.q4cdn.com/435878511/files/doc_presentations/2024/Jun/03/asco-2024-adjuvant-melanoma.pdf)

FIX
Smallest change:
"When the same data were published in The Lancet, they came with two-sided p = 0.053, although the prespecified analysis was one-sided."
That fixes the standards breach without changing the argument.

Suspicions I could not source

One sentence gave me pause:
"The only route to it is a clinical trial."
I verified that intismeran remains investigational and found multiple ongoing clinical trials, but I did not find authoritative Merck/Moderna documentation establishing that expanded-access or compassionate-use access is categorically unavailable. I therefore do not count this as a finding. The stronger claims immediately around it—that the therapy is investigational and not presently an approved treatment—are supported. The Phase 3 announcement itself calls intismeran "investigational."

Otherwise, no findings. In particular, several places I initially scrutinized held up on closer checking: the five-year RFS/DMFS/OS hazard ratios and intervals match the JCO paper; seven deaths occurred in each arm despite the different Kaplan-Meier five-year survival estimates; the three-year paper does in fact report the 95% CI of 0.288–0.906 in its full table even though its abstract foregrounds the 80% CI; and the Phase 3 registry confirms investigator-assessed RFS, double blinding, and OS as a secondary endpoint.

So under the deliberately narrow question you assigned me—what must be fixed before publication?—my answer is one item: the Rule 7 sentence above.

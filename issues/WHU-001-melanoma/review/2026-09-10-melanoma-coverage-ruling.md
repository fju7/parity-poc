# Ruling — melanoma's 212, and the gap it opens in rule 13

---

## 1. What is true, stated at the right register

Overstating this would be its own inaccuracy, in the harsh direction, which is
still inaccuracy. So precisely:

**True.** The 131 sentences that were examined passed. They do name the words they
rest on, and every figure they carry is in one of those spans. That result stands
and nothing about it is in question.

**True.** 212 of melanoma's 343 body sentences were never examined by rule 1 or
rule 2. The function that reported on the other 131 opened with *"Every sentence
on the page, no exemptions."*

**Not true, and must not be said.** That melanoma is unbound, or that anything on
it is false. **Not examined is not failing.** No claim on that page has been
shown wrong by any of this, and the 212 may be entirely sound.

**Also true, and this is the part that matters.** It is not melanoma-specific.
Across all three issues, **412 of 1,256 published body sentences — about a third —
have been examined by the rules that define this publication's method.** That is
the number, and it is now known.

---

## 2. Appendix D omits the largest instance of its own subject

This does not depend on what any page claims. Appendix D exists to record **where
the machinery has not looked.** Two hundred and twelve unexamined sentences is
the largest single instance of that in this publication's existence, and Appendix
D does not contain it.

That is a completeness failure in an appendix defined by completeness, on a live
page. It needs the number, and putting it there requires a republish.

The gate-budget question you settled for the dateline settles this the same way
and more strongly: repairing what a published page discloses is repair of
published work, not new review of it. My view, and it remains yours.

**Before drafting it, one thing must be checked** — the same check I asked for at
site level and have not asked for at page level. What does melanoma's **own**
furniture and appendices say about its binding coverage? Verbatim, with location.
If the page tells a reader that every sentence is bound or declared, that is a
live overstatement and the disclosure is a correction. If it makes a scoped
claim, the disclosure is an addition. Different acts, different log entries, and
I will not guess which.

---

## 3. The finding that outlives the fix: rule 13 rests on something nothing
## produces

You accepted melanoma on 9 September. Rule 13, as rewritten after you refused to
sign the earlier version, says acceptance requires **two things the operator
genuinely can supply — knowing what remains open, and choosing to publish
anyway.**

The list of what remained open, when you signed, was: the S029 erratum. It did
not include *212 of 343 sentences were never examined by rules 1 and 2*, because
nobody knew.

**So the first of rule 13's two requirements was not supplied — not by you, to
you.** That is not a failure of your decision; it is a failure of the information
the decision was made on, and the information was ours to get right.

And it generalises past this instance, which is why it is the finding:

> **Rule 13 places its entire weight on "knowing what remains open", and nothing
> in this apparatus produces or checks that list.** It has been assembled by hand,
> from memory, by whoever was writing the acceptance block. Every failure this
> week has come from a hand-maintained representation drifting from the thing it
> represents — the index dates, the family instance count, the correction log,
> the four-commit figure. The open list is one of those, and it is the one a
> publication decision rests on.

**The fix is the one already applied twice today: derive it.** The acceptance
block's open list must be generated from the checks' own outputs — every
NOT EXAMINED, every NOT EVALUATED, every open errata debt, every waived row —
rather than written from memory. Then "what remains open" is a measurement, and
rule 13's requirement can actually be met.

That is a small piece of work and it closes the gap that rule 13's own first real
test just exposed. Note what has happened twice now: the acceptance mechanism has
been exercised twice, and both times it revealed a defect in itself. That is the
mechanism working, and it should be recorded as such rather than as two
embarrassments.

---

## 4. What follows for the acceptance itself

The 9 September acceptance was made on an incomplete open list. It is not
invalidated retroactively — you decided what you decided, and the decision was
yours to make.

But the disclosure republish needs a **fresh acceptance**, and its open list must
be the derived one, carrying the 212 and the third-of-the-corpus figure. Not
ceremony: it is the first acceptance in this publication's history that will rest
on a list nobody assembled from memory.

---

## 5. WARN not BLOCKED — you were right, and the reason is worth keeping

> *"Reporting it as one would push somebody to delete prose to clear a number,
> which is failure 20 running the other way."*

That is exactly right and it is a better statement of failure 20 than the entry
has. Reasoning from the symptom has two directions: edit the data to make a check
pass, or edit the *page* to make a check pass. The second is worse, because the
page is the product. Add that direction to the entry.

Your self-caught regression — the first version calling `page_sentences()` and
taking the suite from 89 to 61, and the fix reporting "Unknown, not zero" rather
than omitting the row — is the same principle applied to the check's own failure
mode. A row that vanishes on error reintroduces the absence it exists to remove.
Third instance of a new call reaching for a real page on a synthetic slug; the
pattern being recorded is the right output.

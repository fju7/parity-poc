I reviewed the September 4 packet as a fresh article, using the review prompt embedded in it rather than simply checking my prior findings.

The revisions have materially improved the piece., the one-sided framework is handled correctly, the Phase 2b composite endpoints are preserved in most places, the normality of topline announcements is expressly acknowledged, and the treatment of the two placebo-controlled PD-1 trials is much more defensible.

I found nine items I would fix before publication. One is a direct counterexample to a universal negative; two are Appendix A inference failures; the remainder are factual/internal-standard breaches.

## Finding 1

QUOTE

"Morning Glory Sciences — an outlet we can find no other publication citing, so we give the argument on its merits rather than on its authority — …"

BREACH

Rule 11 — Before criticising the coverage, find the best of it; also a factual error.

WHY

There is a direct counterexample. A 2026 letter in the Irish Journal of Medical Science cites Morning Glory Sciences as a reference, and PubMed records that letter as externally peer-reviewed. Because the negative is being used specifically to discount the outlet's standing, this is the kind of Rule 11 problem your standard treats as especially important.

SOURCE

— the reference list includes "Morning Glory Sciences. Oncology drug approval news flash…"; PubMed also states: "Provenance and Peer Review: Not commissioned, externally peer-reviewed."

FIX

Delete: "an outlet we can find no other publication citing, so we give the argument on its merits rather than on its authority".

This counterexample also defeats the negative premise recorded in J28.

## Finding 2

INFERENCE

J01 — "0 Phase 3 efficacy numbers released"

FAILS

The step does not follow from the premises.

WHY

J01's premises establish that the company announcement contains no Phase 3 efficacy figure and that the registry has no results. Its own reasoning then explicitly narrows the zero to "anything we hold." The published card removes that scope and makes a claim about what has been released anywhere. Appendix A itself acknowledges the mismatch.

I did not find a Phase 3 effect-size counterexample in the open web search; recent coverage still says the effect sizes and subgroup data remain undisclosed. That makes the card probably true, but the inference as documented still fails.

FIX

Scope the card to the search, e.g. "0 Phase 3 efficacy numbers found in our sources," or remove the card.

## Finding 3

INFERENCE

J09 — "The single most useful question to ask of any interval: does it cross 1.0?"

FAILS

A premise does not say what the step takes it to say.

WHY

S025 establishes 1.0 as the null value for a hazard ratio, not for confidence intervals generally. Difference measures use 0 as the null value. Because this section is presented as an explanation of confidence intervals rather than merely a caption to one HR chart, "any interval" is factually overbroad.

SOURCE

— "For ratio effect measures, a value of 1 represents no difference … For difference measures, a value of 0 represents no difference."

FIX

Change "any interval" to "any hazard-ratio interval," and correspondingly make the next sentence "For a hazard ratio, 1.0 means no difference."

## Finding 4

QUOTE

"The probability of seeing a result at least this good if the treatment actually did nothing. A small p-value means the result would be a surprising fluke."

BREACH

Factual/statistical error.

WHY

The very next callout gives the more complete definition and says that the other assumptions "are part of the claim, not a footnote to it." The opening definition nevertheless conditions only on treatment ineffectiveness, and "fluke" implies that a small p-value specifically reflects random chance. A small p-value can instead arise because other assumptions are wrong.

SOURCE

— the p-value is defined conditional on "every model assumption" being correct, and the authors stress that it does not identify which assumption is wrong.

FIX

Delete those first two sentences. The correct definition is already given immediately below, so nothing needs to be added.

## Finding 5

QUOTE

"Those newly-included patients start at lower risk of recurrence, which leaves less room for absolute benefit, and Pharmacy Times' counter is that their risk may be closer to stage III than the staging suggests."

BREACH

Rule 9 — Stage and population change the absolute answer.

WHY

This resolves in Morning Glory's favor the disagreement the preceding paragraph just says "cannot be settled from anything published." More importantly, "those newly-included patients" lumps IIB, IIC and IIIA together as lower-risk; large stage-specific data show IIB and IIIA have comparable recurrence risk and IIC and IIIB have comparable recurrence risk. The stage relationship is not monotonic enough to support the blanket statement.

SOURCE

— the nationwide cohort found "comparable risk of recurrence" between IIIA and IIB and between IIIB and IIC.
A 2026 cohort likewise found stage IIC had the highest numerical recurrence rate among IIB–IIIB patients studied, although that particular IIC-vs-IIIB difference was not formally tested.

FIX

Delete that sentence. The following sentence — "Whether the effect holds up across that wider range is a real open question…" — already states the supportable conclusion.

## Finding 7

QUOTE

"If someone quotes '49%' at you, ask which trial. It is a figure from 157 patients in an open-label study, not the result announced this month — and it describes a rate of recurrence, not a share of patients cured."

Also:

"Until it reads out on enough events, the honest sentence is that this therapy reduces recurrence and distant metastasis…"

BREACH

Rule 3 — Preserve composite endpoints.

WHY

The 49% figure is a reduction in the hazard of recurrence or death. The 59% figure is a reduction in the hazard of distant metastasis or death. These two remaining shorthand formulations drop death from the composites after the rest of the article has been carefully repaired to preserve it.

SOURCE

— the Phase 2b figures are explicitly "49% reduction in the risk of recurrence or death" and "59% reduction in the risk of distant metastasis or death."

FIX

Change the first to "a rate of recurrence or death," and the second to "reduces recurrence or death, and distant metastasis or death."

## Not a breach, but you should know

I specifically tested the Appendix C concern about the phrase "in its own voice" for KOL Pulse. I think the qualification survives. KOL Pulse reproduces the oncologist's announcement-day post containing the Phase 2b HRs, but its own editorial material expressly says numerical Phase 3 results are not disclosed and warns that the circulating hazard ratios are Phase 2b figures. That is a meaningful distinction between an outlet's reporting and material it quotes, not an unfalsifiable hedge.

I also did not find a counterexample to the two most exposed Phase 3 negatives: I found no published INTerpath-001 hazard ratio/CI/absolute rate and no stage-specific Phase 3 subgroup result as of September 4, 2026. Several current sources independently say those data remain undisclosed.

The overall assessment is substantially stronger than the September 3 version. The remaining defects are quite localized: two genuinely damaging assertions (Morning Glory and the blanket stage-risk statement), two statistical explainer errors, two internal factual/source errors, one remaining composite-endpoint cleanup, J01's deliberate scope mismatch, and the correction-history dating.
